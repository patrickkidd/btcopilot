# What the inter-rater meetings found

The full meeting records name the clinicians who coded and the cases they coded, so they are kept outside every repo. This file carries only the generic results: what was being calibrated, where coders disagreed, how it was settled, and what stayed open. Every participant is "a coder". The two cases were written for training, and neither is named here.

Six meetings were held between February and April 2026. Four coders took part. The first three meetings worked on the first training case and the last three on the second.

## 2026-02-16: first case, opening statements

**Calibrated.** How to turn a client's opening statements into symptom, anxiety, relationship and functioning shifts, and how to enter them.

**Disagreements and how they were settled.**
- One statement described a change in the speaker and a separate change in a relative. One coder entered a single shift. All agreed it is two shifts, one per person, and the single entry was a data-entry slip.
- A statement where the speaker carries the load a sibling does not was coded three ways: a triangle, over- and under-functioning, and a plain move toward or away. The triangle could not be defended in discussion and was dropped. Over- and under-functioning drew tentative agreement. Toward or away stayed viable as the lowest-inference choice.
- Whether a later statement repeating the same situation is a new shift or a restatement was not settled. The structural data it revealed, a sibling and their shared parents, was agreed.
- For a relative who pulled back, coders split between "away" and "distance", and between which people were the recipients. All agreed on the direction of the behavior, a person becoming less involved. No one mechanism label won.

**Rules that came out of it.**
- Two people changing means two shifts.
- Each new shift is a new event entry. Do not append evidence to an existing event.
- When a sibling first appears, add the shared parents so the sibling link can be drawn.
- When the evidence supports several mechanisms, prefer the code with the fewest theoretical assumptions.
- Prefer "away" over "distance" unless there is evidence the pulling back is driven by anxiety.
- Proposed definition of over-functioning: automatic behavior done for another person where there is room to choose differently.
- The tense of the description should match the date: present tense gets a present date, "six months ago" gets backdated.
- Do not change codings of a case already reviewed in a meeting.

**Stayed open.**
- Mechanism labels at each statement versus agreement on the end result. One coder argued, from earlier coding work, that what matters is whether a shift happened and what it did to anxiety and functioning. The counter-position is that training an extraction model needs explicit statement-level rules.
- Rules have to hold for three uses at once: the reliability study, model training data, and what the apps can store and show.
- The emotional system shows several patterns at once, so one statement can honestly support several codes.
- A heuristic was offered and not adopted as a rule: pick the mechanism whose follow-up question a coach would actually ask next.
- The group named the risk of agreeing too fast with the most experienced voice in the room. Agreement is only useful if it is on the right thing.
- Raw coding data should carry as few theoretical assumptions as possible. Interpretation belongs in a later evaluation pass.

## 2026-02-23: first case, continued

**Calibrated.** The same case, picking up the unresolved statement about a relative pulling back, and a new statement about a parent who lives far away.

**Disagreements and how they were settled.**
- One coder proposed defaulting every relationship shift to toward or away. The group kept the mechanism-specific codes because the tools and the apps are built around them. With more evidence, agreement firmed up: the speaker over-functions and the sibling under-functions. Whether the sibling's pulling back is a separate distance event was set aside.
- A later statement re-mentioned the sibling's avoidance. All agreed it gets no new entry.
- A parent who lives in another region after a divorce was coded by three coders as distance. The group converged on "moved", because physical separation is not evidence of emotional distance.
- One coder entered a "same" value for a relative, and this was only partly discussed.

**Rules that came out of it.**
- Same person, same shift, same time period, mentioned again: no new entry.
- A re-mention that refers to a different time can be a new entry, often with the "same" value.
- "Same" is a second data point that confirms persistence at a new date. It is never a way to count repeat mentions.
- Code a geographic relocation as "moved". Do not infer emotional distance from it.
- When any family member first appears, add both parents, unnamed if need be, so parentage can be recorded.
- The person field holds whoever does the action, such as the distancer or the over-functioner. Recipients go in the recipient fields. The speaker is not the default.
- Automaticity test for any mechanism: does the behavior look automatic rather than chosen?

**Stayed open.**
- Coders tend to reach the same shift at different statements. The group began to ask whether comparing the whole timeline at the end matters more than when each shift was first coded.
- Marking every mention of a theme, and watching the density of mentions build, was offered as a way to see when a shift happens.

## 2026-03-16: first case, reviewed with the new comparison tools

**Calibrated.** Not statement by statement. The group reviewed the whole case through three new tools: a model-written calibration report that groups disagreements by construct, a per-event literature check against Bowen texts, and a side-by-side timeline for any two coders.

**Disagreements and how they were settled.**
- For the relative who pulled back, the literature check sided with distance over conflict. A coder noted that "stepping back" can mean anxious avoidance or a considered step back, so the words alone do not decide it.
- The literature check confirmed over-functioning for the speaker. Its concepts were right but its citations were approximate.
- The tool coined a term no coder recognized. The group chose not to constrain the tool's wording, because each claim can be traced to its source passage.
- A coder absent from the previous meeting was brought up to date on the no-duplicate rule and agreed.

**Rules that came out of it.**
- The whole cumulative timeline is the main unit of comparison. Statement-by-statement review stays as the working method for data entry.
- Code blind first. Use the literature check only afterwards, and treat it like another coder whose view must not shape your own.
- Anxiety and relationship are separate variables that are meant to vary independently.
- Relationship shifts are events, not levels. Symptom, anxiety and functioning rise and fall like a line. A relationship shift is a point in time.

**Stayed open.**
- A formal protocol for using model output in research.
- The calibration report sometimes linked to the wrong event and repeated the same code.

## 2026-03-30: second case, opening statements

**Calibrated.** The second training case, with all four coders coding blind first.

**Disagreements and how they were settled.**
- The presenting complaint was coded as symptom by one coder and as anxiety by two. A fourth coded a defined-self move by the spouse who suggested help, reading it as an ultimatum. The symptom-or-anxiety question was not settled. The defined-self reading was argued, on the grounds that defining self is a move and need not be calm, and the others did not adopt it.
- A statement about a spouse's complaints over late nights at work was split between conflict and distance. One definition offered: conflict is a difference where neither side gives in, including passive resistance and cold standoffs. Distance is avoidance, simply not dealing with it. The other reading saw no sign the speaker was holding a position, only withdrawing. Not settled.
- A statement about freezing and losing focus at home was agreed by all four as functioning down. Most also coded anxiety up.
- Several coders missed an unnamed parent or the spouse's marriage entry on the structural statements.

**Rules that came out of it.**
- When a spouse first appears, add the marriage so the pair-bond exists.
- Functioning down shows as being unsure of oneself: frozen, not knowing what to do, unable to focus, clarity of thought overridden.
- Anxiety up and functioning down often occur together. They are different variables, and when the evidence supports both, code both.
- Date placeholders: year only gets January 1st. Month known gets the 1st of that month. Backdate from the discussion date, which must be set before coding starts.
- The coder's professional interpretation is the contribution. Coding is not underlining what was literally said.

**Stayed open.**
- Where the line between conflict and distance falls. This was bookmarked for a literature search.
- How wide defined self reaches, and whether it can be inferred from one statement.
- Symptom versus anxiety for a presenting complaint.
- Whether to take a speaker's account of another person's motives at face value.
- Whether "anxiety same" is valid for a restatement within one session.

## 2026-04-13: second case, continued

**Calibrated.** Structural statements about a sibling's household, and a statement about a parent who has always been sensitive. Three coders attended.

**Disagreements and how they were settled.**
- A statement that the speaker is not close to a sibling split two to two between distance and nothing. The argument for nothing was that "not close" can mean many things and is not evidence of distance. Not settled.
- For the sensitive parent, one coder coded symptom up. The group rejected it: sensitivity and reactivity are anxiety, not symptom. Functioning down was also rejected, because "always been this way" describes a baseline and not a shift. The statement also held a real timestamped change, when a birth in the family made the sensitivity permanent. No coder had captured it at first.

**Rules that came out of it.**
- A symptom is a functional breakdown, the point where anxiety exceeds the capacity to function. A behavior or trait counts only when it interferes with physical, behavioral or social functioning.
- A baseline ("always been") is coded once, with the date marked uncertain. A shift needs a timestamp. A baseline that changes permanently at a known event is a timestamped shift.
- Structural events are birth, death, marriage, divorce, bonding, moving and adoption. They are entered separately from symptom, anxiety, relationship and functioning shifts, even when they happen at the same time.
- Facts like occupation, schooling, health and location are markers of functioning across generations, not shifts. The data model has no proper home for them yet.
- Do not code functioning for infants. An infant's symptom can be coded.
- A mechanism needs an emotional driver. Helping someone who genuinely cannot manage is responding to reality, not over-functioning.
- Separate episodes of a chronic pattern at different dates are separate entries. The pattern itself is coded once.

**Stayed open.**
- Whether "not close" is ever enough for distance.
- A literature search for Bowen's list of the variables of differentiation.

## 2026-04-27: second case, continued

**Calibrated.** The sensitive-parent statement again, a statement about a parent who withdraws from the household, and a statement about how a parent's attention has shifted since a life change.

**Disagreements and how they were settled.**
- The sensitive parent: all agreed to two entries. One is the baseline anxiety with an uncertain date. The other is anxiety up at the birth that made it permanent. Two different times make two entries, and the no-duplicate rule applies only within one time period.
- The withdrawing parent was coded as distance by all four. One coder also coded a child-focused triangle.
- That raised the largest open question so far. One coder held that anxiety is built into every relationship mechanism, so coding it separately adds nothing for chronic patterns. Another held that anxiety must stay a separately coded variable, otherwise it can never be shown to vary on its own. High correlation between the two is fine. No rule was made.
- The shifting-attention statement: one coder read the speaker as moved from the inside to the outside of a triangle, with grandchildren now inside. Two read the speaker as still inside, with the grandchildren added rather than replacing them. The fourth coded each part separately and leaned toward inside. One coder placed a spouse on the outside as a prediction without textual evidence, and that was not accepted. Not settled.
- All four coders missed a life event, a retirement, that restructured a parent's daily routine.

**Rules that came out of it.**
- One statement can yield two entries, a baseline and a later intensification, when they sit at different times.
- Code only the shift. The earlier established pattern was already coded when it first appeared, and later descriptions of it are restatements.
- A coding placed as a prediction without evidence in the text is not accepted.
- Every coder must code the same case before it is reviewed.

**Stayed open.**
- Whether anxiety is coded separately when a relationship mechanism is coded.
- Triangle position when a new person joins rather than replaces.
- The group felt statement-level review loses the forest for the trees, and agreed on a whole-case summary before moving to the next case.
- Comparing four coders' full timelines at once is hard to read, and the comparison tools were judged weak.

## What the new coding process inherits

**Rules**
- Two people changing means two shifts, each its own entry.
- Never append evidence to an existing event. A new shift is a new event.
- Same person, same shift, same time period, mentioned again: no new entry.
- A re-mention at a different date can be a new entry, usually "same".
- "Same" confirms persistence at a new date and never counts repeat mentions.
- A baseline is coded once with an uncertain date. A shift needs a timestamp.
- A baseline that changes permanently at a known event is a timestamped shift.
- Code only the shift, not the established pattern it moves away from.
- Separate episodes of a chronic pattern at different dates are separate entries.
- Structural events and shifts are separate entries.
- Occupation, schooling, health and location are not shifts.
- Add both parents when anyone first appears, and the marriage when a spouse first appears.
- Code relocation as "moved". Physical distance is not emotional distance.
- The person field holds whoever acts. The speaker is not the default.
- Do not code functioning for infants.
- The tense of the description sets the date. Year only gets January 1st, month only gets the 1st.
- Set the discussion date before coding starts.
- Code blind first, then consult any model or literature tool.
- Every coder codes the same case before review.
- Do not rewrite codings of a case already reviewed.

**Definitions**
- Symptom is a functional breakdown, not a trait, sensitivity or behavior.
- Anxiety and functioning are separate variables that often move in opposite directions together.
- Functioning down is being unsure of oneself: frozen, unfocused, thinking overridden.
- Over-functioning is automatic doing for another where there is room to choose otherwise.
- A mechanism needs an emotional driver. Responding to a real need is not a mechanism.
- "Away" is observable reduced involvement. "Distance" needs evidence of anxious avoidance.
- Relationship shifts are points in time. Symptom, anxiety and functioning are levels.

**Pitfalls**
- Reaching for a triangle when toward, away or over-functioning explains the text.
- Reading emotional distance into a divorce or a move.
- Treating a restatement as a new shift.
- Missing structural people and life events, such as an unnamed parent, a marriage or a retirement.
- Coding a prediction instead of what the text shows.
- Agreeing with the most experienced voice instead of holding a view.
- Judging agreement statement by statement when coders reach the same timeline at different points.

**Still open, carried forward**
- Conflict versus distance.
- Symptom versus anxiety for a presenting complaint.
- Whether anxiety is coded alongside a relationship mechanism.
- How far defined self reaches, and whether one statement is enough.
- Whether to accept a speaker's account of other people's motives.
- Mechanism-specific codes versus plain toward and away as the default.
