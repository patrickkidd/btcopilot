# Triangles: inside and outside (T)

For coders: what the original authors wrote about the inside and outside positions of a triangle, where to find it, and what is decided. Read it for food for thought while coding. It is not a rule book; section 1 says which rules exist.

- Built 2026-09-26 from the sources listed in [`README.md`](README.md). Every quote below was checked word for word against its source file by the quote checker; the check is repeatable.
- This is the public edition: excerpts from confidential sources are left out, and so are the paths to the source files ([`README.md`](README.md)).
- Ids (T1, T2, ...) are stable. Location keys (FE6 L13, BS1 L126, ...) are explained in [`README.md`](README.md).
- Transcripts of recordings are machine transcriptions. A word marked [sic] is as transcribed.
- Bowen's, Kerr's and Havstad's statements are ideas. None was tested with counted data unless the note says so.
- One page, not two. It replaces the old review's two files, `10-inside.md` and `11-outside.md`. Every source defines each position only against the other, inside one triangle (T2, T3, T7). Patrick ruled that "[ruling text — see the private edition]" (status item 2). The two old files shared 7 of their 10 and 11 passages. The one sense of "outside" that is not a triangle position has its own sub-section, 2f.

## Coder summary

Nothing about coding a triangle is ruled. The picture is ruled: three people, an inside pair close together and one outside, and an inside or outside move that shifts them (status items 1 and 2). When to code a triangle, and on what evidence, is open for the IRR group (status items 4 and 13). The facts said and the triangle that theory predicts are kept apart; a predicted triangle is a hypothesis (status item 6).

The passages that matter most:
- T7 and T8: two comfortable insiders and one outsider at low anxiety, and the outsider keeps trying to get in.
- T14, T15 and T20: under high tension the outside becomes the wanted position, so "outside" is not always the loser's place.
- T29: A complains to C about B, C takes A's side, and "B is the new outsider". The person talked about is the outsider, not an insider; the old review had this backwards.
- T47: "close to my mother, little relationship with my father" is read in the context of the triangle, not as two separate facts.
- T49, T53 and T56: "outside" also names a deliberate step out of the family's automatic pattern while staying in contact. That is differentiation, not a triangle position, and the value space does not yet say how to code it (status item 8).

The main off-theory trap: coding a triangle because theory says one must be there. The IRR coders named it themselves: "knowing triangles, you can assume it. But it's not spelled out in the data" (T73). What the texts give a coder to look for is a move by a named person in relation to two others (T29, T35); whether that alone is enough is open.

## 2. What the original authors wrote

### 2a. What a triangle is, and the two positions

#### Bowen

- <a id="T1"></a>**T1** “I chose triangle in order to convey that this concept has specific meaning beyond that implied in triad. The theory states that the triangle, a three-person emotional configuration, is the molecule or the basic building block of any emotional system” Bowen, FTiCP ch16 L143.
- <a id="T2"></a>**T2** “In periods of calm, the triangle is made up of a comfortably close twosome and a less comfortable outsider. The twosome works to preserve the togetherness, lest one become uncomfortable and form a better togetherness elsewhere. The outsider seeks to form a togetherness with one of the twosome” Bowen, FTiCP ch16 L145.
- <a id="T99"></a>**T99** “Most so-called two person relationships are the calm side of an already functioning triangle in which the calmness is maintained at the expense of a negative relationship with the other corner of the triangle.” Bowen, FTiCP ch17 L59. A calm pair is not evidence that no third person is involved. "Most" is uncounted.
- <a id="T3"></a>**T3** “When a triangle is calm, it consists of a twosome that's a togetherness and an outsider. The preferred positions A and B constitute the togetherness. The preferred position is to be a member of the togetherness. And the other position is the outside position.” Bowen, BS1 L126.
- <a id="T4"></a>**T4** “It's never still. It's moving from moment to moment. Over a long period of time, a triangle has patterns that persist over the years.” Bowen, BS1 L135. Two grains: moves from moment to moment, and a pattern over years.
- <a id="T5"></a>**T5** “In addition there are a variety of secondary triangles when two or more may band together for one corner of a triangle for one emotional issue, while the, configuration shifts on another issue.” Bowen, FTiCP ch21 L55. A corner can hold two people acting as one.

#### Kerr

- <a id="T6"></a>**T6** “The triangle is the basic molecule of an emotional system. It is the smallest stable relationship unit.” Kerr, FE6 L5.
- <a id="T7"></a>**T7** “When anxiety in the emotional field of a triangle is low, two people are comfortably close (the insiders) and the third is a less comfortable outsider. This is not a static system; even during calm periods it is in constant motion.” Kerr, FE6 L13.
- <a id="T8"></a>**T8** “The outsider is not idly standing by, but continually attempts to form a togetherness with one of the insiders.” Kerr, FE6 L13. The outsider is active. A calm person who stays out of it is not what "outsider" means here.
- <a id="T9"></a>**T9** “Three people, together for a brief period of time, will invariably gravitate to a process of two insiders and one outsider.” Kerr, FE6 L63.
- <a id="T10"></a>**T10** “A live third person is not required for a triangle. A fantasied relationship, objects, activities, and pets can all function as a corner of a triangle.” Kerr, FE6 L172 (footnote 3).
- <a id="T11"></a>**T11** “The triangle describes the what, how, when, and where of relationships, not the why. ... The assignment of motive is necessarily subjective and not verifiable; the assignment of function can be objective and potentially verifiable.” Kerr, FE6 L7. Who moved, toward whom and when can be coded; why cannot.

#### Patrick (writing and rulings)

- <a id="T12"></a>**T12** “When two people mutually maintain a move toward each other, that dyad is in an "inside" state. When either or both people maintain an away move toward the other, that dyad is in an "outside" state.” Patrick, TDTA L48 (2021). Here inside and outside are states of a pair, not positions of a person.
- <a id="T13"></a>**T13** “Value space: conflict, distance, reciprocal functioning, child-focus, cutoff, inside (triangle), outside (triangle), defined-self.” Patrick, WP L51 (2025).

### 2b. Tension decides which position is wanted

#### Bowen

- <a id="T101"></a>**T101** “When tensions are very high in families and available family triangles are exhausted, the family system triangles in people from outside the family, such as police and social agencies.” Bowen, FTiCP ch10 L137. Moves toward outsiders (school, police, agencies) are moves in the family's triangles. Code them with the outsider as a target.
- <a id="T14"></a>**T14** “In attention state, now, the preferred position is the outside position. That would say lets you two fight and let me stay out of it.” Bowen, BS1 L132 ("In attention state" [sic], in a tension state).
- <a id="T15"></a>**T15** “In periods of stress, the outside position is the most comfortable and most desired position. In stress, each works to get the outside position to escape tension in the twosome.” Bowen, FTiCP ch16 L147.
- <a id="T16"></a>**T16** “Now as tension builds up in a twosome, usually one person is uncomfortable in the twosome. When the tension goes very high in a twosome, then both are uncomfortable. But in a low tension state, it is the uncomfortable one that makes the move.” Bowen, BS1 L147.

#### Kerr

- <a id="T17"></a>**T17** “The triangle describes the dynamic equilibrium of a three-person system. The major influence on the activity of a triangle is anxiety.” Kerr, FE6 L9. A claim about cause. An anxiety shift still needs its own evidence ([`anxiety.md`](anxiety.md), status item 6).
- <a id="T18"></a>**T18** “In unusually quiet periods, a triangle may be so inactive that its basic relationship processes are not observable. ... Triangles are most easily observed during periods of moderate tension.” Kerr, FE6 L11. No visible triangle in a calm period does not show there is none.
- <a id="T19"></a>**T19** “In a calm emotional field, the insiders work to exclude an outsider; in a moderate anxiety field, the outsider is actively recruited for more involvement.” Kerr, FE6 L15.
- <a id="T20"></a>**T20** “During high stress periods, the emotional process in a triangle assumes new characteristics. Now the outside position is the most comfortable and desired.” Kerr, FE6 L26.
- <a id="T21"></a>**T21** “If anxiety is very low, even in a poorly differentiated family it is possible for the three members of a triangle to function as emotionally separate individuals.” Kerr, FE6 L34.

#### Patrick (writing and rulings)

- <a id="T22"></a>**T22** “3) simultaneously move away from both of the other people. Becoming quiet, submissive, or a "follower" to both of the other two people are examples of two simultaneous away moves. This third option is desirable when the tension is very high.” Patrick, TDTA L46 (2021). Going quiet with both others counts as a move.

### 2c. How a triangle move shows: who moves, and who is talked about

#### Bowen

- <a id="T98"></a>**T98** “The twosome might 'reach out' and pull in the other person, the emotions might 'overflow' to the third person, or the third person might be emotionally programmed to initiate the involvement.” Bowen, FTiCP ch17 L57. The mover can be the third person, not only one of the pair. Code who moved.
- <a id="T24"></a>**T24** “A is the uncomfortable one. And then predictably, A will tell B a story about a mutual friend C, a negative story. ... Now A tells B a story about C. That makes these two buddy buddies.” Bowen, BS1 L166. The two who talk are the insiders; the one talked about is outside.
- <a id="T25"></a>**T25** “We two understand each other and now we're going to talk about that character over there.” Bowen, BS1 L173.
- <a id="T26"></a>**T26** “for the uncomfortable one to "triangle in" a third person by telling the second person a story about the triangle one. This relieves the tension between the first two, and shifts the tension between the second and third.” Bowen, FTiCP ch21 L53.
- <a id="T27"></a>**T27** “each one constantly moving to gain a little more close comfort or to withdraw from tension, with each move by one requiring a compensatory move by another.” Bowen, FTiCP ch21 L53. A move by one is expected to bring a move by another; look for the second move.

#### Kerr

- <a id="T28"></a>**T28** “The uncomfortable one attempts first to restore a comfortable equilibrium with the other insider ... When unsuccessful at restoring comfortable equilibrium, the uncomfortable one will initiate a togetherness with the outsider. The outsider, eager for this, responds quickly to the invitation.” Kerr, FE6 L15. An order of steps from theory; a hypothesis until the dates show it.
- <a id="T29"></a>**T29** “The uncomfortable insider (A) can pull the outsider (C) into the situation through complaints to him about the other insider (B). If C responds sympathetically, taking sides with A, a comfortable closeness (based on undifferentiation) is established between A and C. B is the new outsider. The key element is side-taking.” Kerr, FE6 L17. B, the one complained about, ends up outside.
- <a id="T30"></a>**T30** “A twosome can also involve a third person in its conflict simply by allowing this person to be within earshot. The problem sort of "overflows" onto him.” Kerr, FE6 L17. No invitation is needed.
- <a id="T31"></a>**T31** “the conflict has shifted out of the original twosome and into the relationship between B and C. The tension is decreased between A and B.” Kerr, FE6 L24 (Figure 8). After the move the conflict runs between the new outsider and an insider.
- <a id="T32"></a>**T32** “She then makes predictable moves to gain an inside position such as, "You care more about your mother than you care about me!"” Kerr, FE6 L26. A remark can be the move.
- <a id="T33"></a>**T33** “If person A expresses to person C that he is angry at person B, this communication functions to increase the level of togetherness between A and C. ... In addition, A's anger at B functions to maintain distance between A and B” Kerr, FE6 L49. One remark, two functions: toward C and away from B.
- <a id="T34"></a>**T34** “More subtle triangling messages are communicated by facial expressions, tones of voice, changes in body posture, and other nonverbal signals.” Kerr, FE6 L98. A transcript loses these.

#### Havstad

- <a id="T35"></a>**T35** “Nineteen dieted to goal weight following shifts in active triangles in their nuclear or extended families in which they increased emotional distance from one family member as they increased closeness with another.” Havstad and Sheffield, ART L43. Her working test of a triangle shift: away from one person and toward another, together.

### 2d. Patrick's definition of a triangle, 2019 to 2025

Each entry changed the one before it. None is ruled as the coding rule (status items 4 and 11).

#### Patrick (writing and rulings)

- <a id="T36"></a>**T36** “there should be no notion of triangles, anxiety binding mechanisms, processes of projection, or multigenerational transmission found in the model itself.” Patrick, PiN L111 (2019). 2019: a triangle is something a model should produce, never something coded in.
- <a id="T37"></a>**T37** “"Triangle": (A toward B) necessitates (A away C), (B away C).” Patrick, CD L37 (2020-10-23). 2020: a triangle is read off three toward and away moves.
- <a id="T38"></a>**T38** “When one of the people in a dyad pulls in a third, this can occur in a limited set of ways. They can: 1) move away from the other person in the dyad and toward the third, or 2) move toward the other person in the dyad and away from the third person” Patrick, TDTA L46 (2021). The third way is T22.
- <a id="T39"></a>**T39** “First, an uncomfortable person seeks a mutually positive feeling with another in relation to a third through a toward move. Second, an uncomfortable person seeks a non-mutual negative position in relation to two others with a simultaneous away.” Patrick, EEM L23 (2021).
- <a id="T40"></a>**T40** “A triangle is defined as one move that is simultaneously toward one and away from another. Not all moves are a triangle.” Patrick, LOG L51 (2021). 2021: one move, two directions.
- <a id="T41"></a>**T41** “Example problems are how to measure the level of anxiety/tension required for a triangle to occur, reliability of the definitions used for inside/outside positions” Patrick, IND L77 (2022).

### 2e. What a person's own account shows and hides

#### Bowen

- <a id="T43"></a>**T43** “How many times have you listened clinically to people talk about marriages and you hear them say, one spouse say, I've been uncomfortable for years. And the others say, it's been wonderful, I never knew there was anything wrong.” Bowen, BS1 L150. Two insiders can describe the same years in opposite words.

#### Kerr

- <a id="T44"></a>**T44** “A husband, when on the outside (in fact or fantasy) of the relationship between his wife and oldest daughter, becomes sullen.” Kerr, FE6 L13. "In fact or fantasy": Kerr counts an outside position as perceived.
- <a id="T45"></a>**T45** “The one who is still comfortable may be largely oblivious to the other's unhappiness” Kerr, FE6 L15.
- <a id="T46"></a>**T46** “Family myths about who was a "favorite" or who was an "outsider" are also notoriously unreliable indicators of what actually transpired between a child and his family.” Kerr, FE7 L231.
- <a id="T47"></a>**T47** “If a person says, "I am close to my mother, but have little relationship with my father," such a statement must be interpreted in the context of triangles. The apparent "closeness" with mother and apparent cutoff from father may reflect the father's being in the outside position between his wife and child.” Kerr, FE10 L19.
- <a id="T48"></a>**T48** “Remember that whenever motives are assigned to the behavior of any person in a triangle, awareness of a process that transcends the motives of individuals is lost.” Kerr, FE6 L79.

### 2f. Two senses of "outside"

The texts use "outside" for two things: the third in a triangle, often the rejected one (everything above), and a deliberate step out of the family's automatic pattern while staying in contact with everyone (below). Status item 8.

#### Bowen

- <a id="T49"></a>**T49** “The process of being able to observe is the slow beginning toward moving one small step toward getting one's self "outside" an emotional system. It is only when one can get a little outside that it is possible to begin to observe and to begin to modify an emotional system.” Bowen, FTiCP ch21 L57. The second sense.
- <a id="T50"></a>**T50** “I discovered that when one person could stay emotionally detached from but in contact with the other two people in the triangle, the anxiety between them disappeared” Bowen, BKIS-04-pt1 @00:21:18.
- <a id="T51"></a>**T51** “If I helped her it would have been a mess. So I carefully kept my head out of it. And you know I kept an outside position.” Bowen (probable), BKIS-12-pt2 @00:23:06. On staying out of his wife's dealings with their daughters: the second sense, in Bowen's own words.

#### Kerr

- <a id="T52"></a>**T52** “It only requires that the third person be in adequate emotional contact with the other two and able to remain emotionally separate from them. The process of being in contact and emotionally separate is referred to as "detriangling."” Kerr, FE6 L71.
- <a id="T53"></a>**T53** “Again, the tone of voice and facial expression that accompanies such a remark can make the difference in conveying that one is inside or outside the triangle.” Kerr, FE6 L120. "Outside the triangle" here means detriangled: the second sense, in the words of the first.
- <a id="T54"></a>**T54** “Well differentiated people do not make a "federal case" out of being an outsider, nor does their emotional security depend on being one of the insiders.” Kerr, FE6 L63.

#### Havstad

- <a id="T55"></a>**T55** “One of the central principles of differentiation of self is the ability to be in emotional contact with everyone in the system without taking sides when the family polarizes around an emotional issue.” Havstad, HD p283 (2019 chapter).
- <a id="T56"></a>**T56** “The rejection and pathologizing of the daughter automatically put her alone in an outside position while everyone in the family emotionally banded together. The mother deliberately chose an outside position by separating herself from the family's view that the problem was in the daughter.” Havstad, HD p283 (2019 chapter; "automatically" and "deliberately" in italics in print). Both senses, two people, one family.


### 2g. Order and timing: claims, never defaults

#### Bowen

- <a id="T58"></a>**T58** “Patterns vary, but one of the most common is basic tension between the parents, with the father's gaining the outside position-often being called passive, weak, and distant -leaving the conflict between mother and child.” Bowen, FTiCP ch16 L151. "Passive, weak, and distant" are the labels others give the one outside.

#### Kerr

- <a id="T59"></a>**T59** “(2) A stable twosome can be destabilized by the removal of a third person. For example, marital disharmony may increase after a child leaves home.” Kerr, FE6 L30. An arrival or a departure is a dated event; a change in positions after it still has to be reported.
- <a id="T60"></a>**T60** “This changes the character of the original twosome and can result in the previously more comfortable member ending up in a very uncomfortable outside position. He may become symptomatic at this point.” Kerr, FE6 L75. "May": a claim, not a rule.
- <a id="T61"></a>**T61** “when a person moves from an outside to an inside position in a triangle and experiences a marked increase in his sense of well-being and a decrease in suicidal fantasies and anxiety laden dreams.” Kerr, FE6 L49. A position change and an anxiety shift, reported together. Each needs its own evidence.

#### Patrick (writing and rulings)

- <a id="T62"></a>**T62** “A person consistently in an outside position more than the others in the unit will be more vulnerable to problems of all kinds.” Patrick, TDTA L54 (2021). The most direct relationship-to-symptom claim the records could test. Untested.
- <a id="T63"></a>**T63** “A basic proposition of Bowen theory is that triangles form to contain rising tension. ... How does one record the level of tension in a manner that predicts triangling.” Patrick, LOG L69 (2021). He leaves the question open.

## 4. Worked examples

Each example gives the facts as said, then the questions a coder faces. The questions are left open where the rulings leave them open.

### 4a. Kerr's A reaches her dead father's family through a cousin (FE ch6)

#### Kerr

- <a id="T88"></a>**T88** “A and her mother were the cozy insiders and her father was in the outside position.” Kerr, FE6 L133.
- <a id="T89"></a>**T89** “By making emotional contact with the cousin, A activates the original triangle with her parents. ... The family will respond to A's contact with her cousin as if she had made contact with her father.” Kerr, FE6 L142.
- <a id="T90"></a>**T90** “Remarks by the mother, such as, "Those people are not worth seeing," or, "If you continue to see your cousin, our relationship will never be the same," would be evidence of the disturbance.” Kerr, FE6 L144.

#### Questions

The positions in T88 are Kerr's summary of years of growing up: a baseline, not a dated shift. Contact with the cousin is a move by A that can be dated. The mother's remarks are the dated response Kerr counts as evidence. The father is dead and still a corner (T10). Which of these is a fact for the record, and which is Kerr's reading of it (status item 6)?

### 4b. Kerr's father with lung cancer after the older daughter left (FE ch9)

#### Questions

The order is dated: a birth, the mother's first visit in years, then the accusation. Is the visit a move by the mother toward the youngest daughter that leaves E outside? The accusation is a conflict move between the sisters. Under R-0288 a conflict is coded as its own two-person move, not as the triangle (status item 1).

## 5. Readings that look right but are off-theory

| The reading | What the sources say instead | Where |
|---|---|---|
| The person being talked about is inside (the old review's marker). | The two who talk are the insiders; the one talked about, complained about or blamed is the outsider. | T24, T25, T26, T29 |
| Conflict marks the inside pair. | After a triangle move the conflict runs between the new outsider and an insider. R-0288 keeps conflict a separate two-person move. | T31; status item 1 |
| The calm, neutral one who stays out of it is the outsider. | The triangle outsider is "not idly standing by" but trying to get in. Calm and in contact with both is the second sense of outside, or detriangling. | T8, T49, T52, T56 |
| Outside is always the bad place. | Under high tension the outside is the wanted position. | T14, T15, T20 |
| "I'm close to my mother, not to my father" is two separate facts about two relationships. | Read it in the context of the triangle; the closeness and the distance may be one pattern. | T47 |
| A person who says she was the outsider, or the favourite, is reporting a fact. | Family myths about favourites and outsiders are "notoriously unreliable". Record what was done, and when. | T46 |
| Theory says a triangle must be there, so code it. | Code what is said; a predicted triangle is a labelled hypothesis. | status item 6; T73, T75 |
| "Triangulation" as the term. | Bowen: "I have never used the term, 'triangulation' in any of my writing." | T65; status item 12 |
| A triangle needs three living people. | A dead parent, an object or an activity can be a corner. | T10; example 4a |
| Why she did it (jealousy, loyalty) is part of the code. | The triangle is the what, how, when and where, not the why. | T11, T48 |
