# Cutoff (CO)

For coders: what the original authors wrote about emotional cutoff, where to find it, and what is decided. Read it for food for thought while coding. It is not a rule book; section 1 says which rules exist.

- Built 2026-09-26 from the sources listed in [`README.md`](README.md). Every quote below was checked word for word against its source file by the quote checker; the check is repeatable.
- This is the public edition: excerpts from confidential sources are left out, and so are the paths to the source files ([`README.md`](README.md)).
- Ids (CO1, CO2, ...) are stable. Location keys (FE9 L78, BS7 L654, ...) are explained in [`README.md`](README.md).
- Transcripts of recordings are machine transcriptions. A word marked [sic] is as transcribed.
- Related pages: [`distance.md`](distance.md) holds the passages on distance, away and moved; this page does not repeat them. [`conflict.md`](conflict.md) C30 is Kerr on conflict with in-laws ending in a cutoff.

## Coder summary

Cutoff is in the white paper's list of relationship moves as its own kind, and the picture must show "who is cut off from" whom (status items 2 and 3). Whether cutoff is a kind of move at all, or distance between generations, or a reading of a whole period of contact, is undecided and open for the IRR group (status item 4). Kerr says "Cutoff and distance mean the same thing" (CO7). Who can cut off from whom is also open (status item 5).

The passages that matter most:
- CO7: Kerr, cutoff between the generations "was originally described by the concept of emotional distance"; it was made a separate concept "to emphasize its importance".
- CO14: cutoff is judged on the quality of emotional contact, not on physical distance.
- CO13: many contacts do not mean little cutoff; "Contacts may be frequent, but highly ritualized."
- CO35: cutoff can lower anxiety and can raise it; it is not "good" or "bad".
- CO21: Bowen, people who are cut off can look socially successful.

The main off-theory trap: reading cutoff from geography or from hostile words ("toxic", "dead to me"), and reading a break with the family as healthy independence. The sources say the opposite on each count (CO12, CO21, CO25).

## 1. Status of the code

### Patrick (writing and rulings)

1. **Record a shift, never a level.** RULED. Ruling R-0054 (records shifts, not levels) — see the private edition. Kerr's 0 to 5 cutoff rating is a level (CO15); a record holds the dated start or end of a cutoff.
2. **The picture shows who is cut off from whom.** RULED. Ruling R-0137 (the picture must show who is cut off from whom) — see the private edition. So a cutoff code carries a mover and a target (Claude's reading, not ruled).
3. **Cutoff is its own kind in the white paper's list.** Patrick's writing, not a ruling ([`conflict.md`](conflict.md) C8).
4. **Distance and cutoff: one kind of move or two?** UNDECIDED, for the IRR group (OPEN_QUESTIONS 11). Ruling OPEN_QUESTIONS 11 (coding judgment calls are left to the IRR group) — see the private edition. The options in the sources:
   - (a) one kind, distance, with cutoff meaning distance between generations (Kerr, CO7);
   - (b) two kinds (the white paper; R-0137);
   - (c) cutoff as a reading of a whole period, the quality of contact over years, not a single move (CO14, CO15).
5. **Who can cut off from whom?** OPEN. Kerr: parents cut off from their adult children as much as the reverse (CO33). Bowen uses the word between spouses and from a husband after a birth (CO29, CO30).
6. **Cutoff is judged on the quality of contact.** A source idea, not a ruling: physical distance and the number of contacts are "not a reliable indicator" (CO12, CO13, CO14). What in a record shows quality of contact is not ruled.
7. **Where the kind of move is open, record it in the source's words.** Claude's proposal, not ruled: “record the move in the source's words with mover, target and date, and mark its kind open rather than picking one” CODING section 8.
8. **A cutoff code does not imply an anxiety shift.** RULED 2026-09-26 ([`anxiety.md`](anxiety.md) status item 6). Kerr: cutoff can lower anxiety or raise it (CO35).
9. **A reading from theory stays apart from the facts.** RULED 2026-09-26. See the private corpus for the app's decision on this point (2026-09-26). (OPEN_QUESTIONS 5). "The cutoff made the marriage more intense" or "the contact eased the symptom" is kept, if at all, as a labelled hypothesis (CO39 to CO43).


## 2. What the original authors wrote

### 2a. What cutoff is

#### Bowen

- <a id="CO1"></a>**CO1** “This concept was added to the theory in 1975 after having been a poorly defined extension of other concepts for several years. It was accorded the status of a separate concept to include details not stated elsewhere, and to have a separate concept for emotional process between the generations.” Bowen, FTiCP ch16 L189.
- <a id="CO2"></a>**CO2** “Much thought went into the selection of a term to best describe this process of separation, isolation, withdrawal, running away, or denying the importance of the parental family.” Bowen, FTiCP ch16 L189. The behaviours the word covers.
- <a id="CO3"></a>**CO3** “The unresolved attachment is handled by the intrapsychic process of denial and isolation of self while living close to the parents; or by physically running away; or by a combination of emotional isolation and physical distance.” Bowen, FTiCP ch16 L191. Cutoff can happen while living next door.
- <a id="CO4"></a>**CO4** “We all live in some degree of unresolved emotional attachment to our families of origin. This is handled by the emotional cutoff, either by physical distance or emotional distance. The degree of cutoff gives a rough estimate of the level of differentiation in self.” Bowen, BKIS-06-pt2a @00:01:12.
- <a id="CO5"></a>**CO5** “in a cutoff there couldn't be a cutoff unless self plays a part in it and one just does not go around and stop cutting off from people because cutting off from people is your lifeline which enables you to live and adapt and adjust” Bowen, BKIS-05-pt1b @00:01:49. Cutoff as a way of living and adapting, not only a failure.

#### Kerr

- <a id="CO6"></a>**CO6** “Emotional cutoff is a concept in systems theory that describes the way people manage the undifferentiation (and emotional intensity associated with it) that exists between the generations.” Kerr, FE9 L78.
- <a id="CO7"></a>**CO7** “Cutoff between the generations was originally described by the concept of emotional distance. Cutoff and distance mean the same thing. Cutoff was made a separate concept in the theory to emphasize its importance” Kerr, FE9 L136 (footnote 10). The basis for option (a) in status item 4.
- <a id="CO8"></a>**CO8** “People manage the unresolved attachments through varying degrees of emotional cutoff. Cutoff is accomplished through physical and/or emotional distance.” Kerr, FE10 L53.
- <a id="CO9"></a>**CO9** “While the act of cutting off from others is, in part, an automatic emotional process, there is also an element of conscious choice.” Kerr, FE10 L53. The white paper defines relationship moves as "without choice" ([`conflict.md`](conflict.md) C9).

### 2b. Degrees, and how cutoff is judged

#### Bowen

- <a id="CO19"></a>**CO19** “There are all gradations of the emotional cutoff. An average family situation in our society today is one in which people maintain a distant and formal relationship with the families of origin, returning home for duty visits at infrequent intervals.” Bowen, FTiCP ch16 L195.

#### Kerr

- <a id="CO11"></a>**CO11** “Individuals and nuclear families are cut off emotionally from their families of origin in degrees ranging from minor and insignificant to major and quite significant.” Kerr, FE9 L76.
- <a id="CO12"></a>**CO12** “The amount of physical distance that exists between people and their families of origin is not equivalent to the amount of emotional distance that exists.” Kerr, FE9 L78.
- <a id="CO13"></a>**CO13** “In addition, the number of contacts people have with family does not necessarily reflect the degree of cutoff. Contacts may be frequent, but highly ritualized.” Kerr, FE9 L78. A count of calls or visits does not measure cutoff.
- <a id="CO18"></a>**CO18** “So it is not as if some people are cut off from their families of origin and others are not. It is a matter of degree.” Kerr, FE9 L86.
- <a id="CO17"></a>**CO17** “In the poorly differentiated "cohesive" families, people may interact with one another regularly, but the level of emotional reactivity is often sufficiently high that people are isolated emotionally even though they are in contact physically.” Kerr, FE9 L90.
- <a id="CO14"></a>**CO14** “So physical distance or proximity is not a reliable indicator of emotional cutoff. Cutoff is evaluated on information about the quality of emotional contact between people.” Kerr, FE10 L53.
- <a id="CO15"></a>**CO15** “A person whose emotional cutoff is rated at "4" is in some contact with at least part of the family, but his participation in family matters is infrequent, superficial, and unpredictable.” Kerr, FE10 L53. A rating of a level, which SARF does not record (status item 1).
- <a id="CO16"></a>**CO16** “These "semi-profiles" of people who are more or less cut off from their families are woefully incomplete.” Kerr, FE10 L53. Kerr's own caveat on his scale.

### 2c. How cutoff shows, and what hides it

#### Bowen

- <a id="CO22"></a>**CO22** “The person who runs away from his family of origin is as emotionally dependent as the one who never leaves home. They both need emotional closeness, but they are allergic to it.” Bowen, FTiCP ch16 L193.

#### Kerr

- <a id="CO24"></a>**CO24** “Many people "escape" their families of origin determined to be different from them. These are often the most cutoff people of all. They marry, invest strongly in their "new" family” Kerr, FE9 L80.
- <a id="CO25"></a>**CO25** “Declaring one's "independence" from family is not differentiation of self. It does not resolve one's emotional fusion with the past. Most people who claim to be "independent" of their families have "broken away" from them rather than "grown away" from them.” Kerr, FE9 L82.
- <a id="CO28"></a>**CO28** “A person who cuts off from his family of origin and "joins" his spouse's family often becomes an "emotional appendage" of the spouse's family.” Kerr, FE9 L88. Close contact with in-laws can go with cutoff from one's own family.
- <a id="CO26"></a>**CO26** “It is easier to avoid people one has difficulty dealing with than it is to deal with them. It is easy to justify the avoidance on the basis of one's family being "impossible."” Kerr, FE10 L53. "My family is impossible" is a statement to record, not the cutoff itself.

### 2d. Who cuts off from whom

#### Bowen

- <a id="CO31"></a>**CO31** “Generally it breaks down at about adolescence and that's the time that the child begins to see the parent as a square or they're mean or what have you. And then the parent starts nagging and there's a relationship cut off between.” Bowen, BS1 L577.
- <a id="CO30"></a>**CO30** “The usual reaction when this happens is a husband gets mad and cuts himself off and goes out into relationships over here.” Bowen, BS2 L258. Cutoff between spouses after a birth.
- <a id="CO29"></a>**CO29** “So the average marriage has its list of taboo subjects that neither mentions to the other lest it upset the other, and if the other's upset then self is upset. So begins the communication cutoff between spouses.” Bowen, BS6 L57.
- <a id="CO32"></a>**CO32** “if you're trying to relate back to a side of the family that is cut off from you and doesn't care to see you then that's the two sides of the cutoff” Bowen, BKIS-05-pt1b @00:02:07.

#### Kerr

- <a id="CO33"></a>**CO33** “The greater the undifferentiation or fusion between the generations, the greater the likelihood the generations will cut off from one another. Parents often cut off from their adult children as much as their adult children cut off from them.” Kerr, FE9 L78.
- <a id="CO34"></a>**CO34** “Cutoff may "solve" the immediate problem (reduce marital conflict), but when spouses pressure one another to cut off from extended family, they are doing the same thing to each other that they accuse the parents or in-laws of doing.” Kerr, FE7 L134.

### 2e. What cutoff does: claims

[`anxiety.md`](anxiety.md) status item 8 applies here too: record the order of the dates, and keep any direction as a hypothesis.

#### Bowen

- <a id="CO40"></a>**CO40** “The spouse who separates from his family of origin does not resolve the emotional attachment. The old relationship remains "latent" and can be revived with emotional contact.” Bowen, FTiCP ch9 L71.
- <a id="CO41"></a>**CO41** “There are other nuclear families in which both spouses detach themselves from families of origin. In these the spouses are usually much more dependent on each other, and the emotional process in the family tends to be more intense.” Bowen, FTiCP ch9 L71.
- <a id="CO67"></a>**CO67** “In general, the more a nuclear family is emotionally cut off from parental families, the higher its incidence of problems and symtoms.” Bowen, FTiCP ch12 L21. A cross-family correlation, stated without a sample. [sic] "symtoms".
- <a id="CO42"></a>**CO42** “The more intense the cutoff with the past, the more likely the individual to have an exaggerated version of his parental family problem in his own marriage, and the more likely his own children to do a more intense cutoff with him in the next generation.” Bowen, FTiCP ch16 L191.
- <a id="CO43"></a>**CO43** “If one or both parents can reestablish emotional contact with their families of origin, the anxiety level subsides, the symptoms become softer and more manageable, and family therapy can become productive.” Bowen, FTiCP ch16 L195.
- <a id="CO68"></a>**CO68** “Those who use physical distance in 'cutting-off' from the parents tend to have the most intense relationships with those outside the family.” Bowen, FTiCP ch20 L3. After a cutoff, look for new intensity at work or with friends; code those moves with their own targets.
- <a id="CO39"></a>**CO39** “The more these two people stay cut off from this old intense relationship they had, the more intense the symptoms in whatever happens here. are. Now, that doesn't change the basic degree on the scale. In other words, the basic level is the same but the symptoms are greater.” Bowen, BS2 L2274.

#### Kerr

- <a id="CO38"></a>**CO38** “Even if a parent lives far away from his parents and sees them infrequently, the death of one or both of his parents can have a major impact on the level of anxiety within him and within his nuclear family.” Kerr, FE9 L74. A death in a family one sees rarely is still an event to record.
- <a id="CO35"></a>**CO35** “Cutoff can reduce anxiety and should not be regarded as "good" or "bad." However, while people and nuclear families can reduce anxiety by cutting off, people and nuclear families can also increase anxiety by cutting off.” Kerr, FE9 L76.
- <a id="CO36"></a>**CO36** “Cutoff may relieve immediate pressure and lower anxiety, but the person's basic vulnerabilities to intense relationships remain unchanged.” Kerr, FE9 L80.
- <a id="CO37"></a>**CO37** “People who are significantly cut off from their families of origin and who develop serious symptoms may get complete or at least partial remission of those symptoms based on reestablishing contact with the family. Reestablishing contact is not a matter of a few phone calls to parents or a few trips home.” Kerr, FE9 L94.

### 2f. Bridging a cutoff

#### Bowen

- <a id="CO44"></a>**CO44** “would be a triangle with a mother with the negative side of the triangle between two siblings who would then have nothing to do with each other from then on. and I've seen these that would go for a hundred years” Bowen, BKIS-05-pt1b @00:11:54. A cutoff can outlive everyone in it; a descendant's contact is the dated event.
- <a id="CO45"></a>**CO45** “Some people are very anxious about returning to their families. Without systems coaching, they can make the problem worse. Others can return, continue the same emotional isolation they used when they were in the family, and accomplish nothing.” Bowen, FTiCP ch16 L195. A visit home is not by itself a bridged cutoff.

#### Kerr

- <a id="CO27"></a>**CO27** “People rarely reduce cutoff by being "more honest" with their families or by "confronting" them.” Kerr, FE9 L86. A confrontation is a move of its own, not a bridge.
- <a id="CO46"></a>**CO46** “A person's effort to work on his functioning in relationship to his family of origin will initially disturb the balance of emotional forces in his nuclear family. This may result in a short-term exacerbation of symptoms in himself or others” Kerr, FE9 L137 (footnote 12). Symptoms up after contact is a claimed pattern, not a sign the contact failed.

## 4. Worked examples

Each example gives the facts as said, then the questions a coder faces. The questions are left open where the rulings leave them open.

### 4a. K.'s family (Basic Series 7, "Emotional Cutoff: A Clinical Example")

A published teaching tape; family members are named here by initial only (case card bowen-k-family).

#### Bowen

- <a id="CO60"></a>**CO60** “The mother's family and the father's family are both from Georgia, which is a fair distance away. They're real cut off.” Bowen, BS7 L11. Distance and cutoff named in one breath.
- <a id="CO21"></a>**CO21** “And this would be the people who are cut off will make a big thing out of their social relationships.” Bowen, BS7 L654. Said of K.'s mother, who does well socially. Cutoff can look like social success.
- <a id="CO20"></a>**CO20** “In other words, it wasn't so much of a negative cutoff, as far as I could tell, except they just had no more contact. ... So that would be another kind of a cutoff.” Bowen, BS7 L823. Orphaned siblings dispersed a century earlier: cutoff without hostility.
- <a id="CO61"></a>**CO61** “In other words, there's the cut off. That's in me. I built the cut off.” K. and Bowen (the transcript has no speaker labels), BS7 L847. The cutoff located in K. herself, not only in her family.

#### Questions

Which of these is a dated move? The parents' distance from their families of origin is a baseline across a marriage. The siblings' loss of contact a century back is a cutoff with no date and no conflict. K.'s own work to reduce the cutoff runs over years: one event, a series of toward moves, or a functioning shift? Not ruled. Nothing on the tape measures quality of contact (status item 6).


## 5. Readings that look right but are off-theory

| The reading | What the sources say instead | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Moving far away means cut off; living nearby means not. | Physical distance "is not a reliable indicator"; people can be cut off in the same house. | CO3, CO12, CO14, CO17 |
| Frequent calls and visits mean no cutoff. | "Contacts may be frequent, but highly ritualized." | CO13 |
| Cutoff sounds hostile: "toxic", "dead to me". | Cutoff can look like charm and social success, or a quiet loss of contact. | CO20, CO21 |
| Cutting off a difficult family is healthy boundary-setting. | "Broken away" is not "grown away"; cutoff is neither good nor bad; the vulnerabilities stay. | CO25, CO35, CO36 |
| Honesty or confrontation repairs a cutoff. | People "rarely reduce cutoff" that way. | CO27 |
| A trip home or a few calls bridge a cutoff. | Contact "is based on a consistent effort made over a long period"; some return and "accomplish nothing". | CO37, CO45 |
| Cutoff is always the child's move against the parents. | Parents cut off as much as children; Bowen uses the word between spouses. Open. | CO29, CO33; status item 5 |
| Cutoff and distance are settled as two separate kinds. | Kerr: "Cutoff and distance mean the same thing." Undecided. | CO7; status item 4 |
| Contact that brings symptoms means the bridging failed. | A short-term rise in symptoms is Kerr's expected first step. | CO46 |
| Cutoff means anxiety went down (or up), so code both. | Ruled out 2026-09-26: anxiety needs its own evidence. Cutoff can move it either way. | status item 8; CO35 |
