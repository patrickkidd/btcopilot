# Projection: focus on a child (P)

For coders: what the original authors wrote about the family projection process, where to find it, and what is decided. Read it for food for thought while coding. It is not a rule book; section 1 says which rules exist.

- Built 2026-09-26 from the sources listed in [`README.md`](README.md). Every quote below was checked word for word against its source file by the quote checker; the check is repeatable.
- This is the public edition: excerpts from confidential sources are left out, and so are the paths to the source files ([`README.md`](README.md)).
- Ids (P1, P2, ...) are stable. Location keys (FTiCP ch16 L185, IRR M6 @00:19:11, ...) are explained in [`README.md`](README.md).
- Transcripts of recordings are machine transcriptions. A word marked [sic] is as transcribed.
- Bowen's, Kerr's and Havstad's statements are ideas. None was tested with counted data unless the note says so.
- Patrick's value space calls this kind of move "child-focus" (P12); Bowen's name is the family projection process.

## Coder summary

Projection is coded without asking the person, because it is "too automatic for people to know" (status item 1). What is open is the direction. Patrick ruled out coding it only when a problem in the child came first; it is undecided whether it is also coded when the parent's anxiety came first, or coded with no direction and the order recorded separately (status item 2). A projection that theory predicts but nobody reported stays a labelled hypothesis, never a fact (status item 3). What counts as enough evidence is open (status item 7).

The passages that matter most:
- P32 and P33: Bowen: the process "begins with anxiety in the mother", then, once started, can be driven by either side.
- P40 and P41: Kerr: once entrenched it "can be activated by either mother or child"; "Who gets anxious first is less important".
- P31: Bowen: most often the parents initiate it, "Or the child may initiate the projection".
- P14 to P16: the steps a coder can see: a feeling, a diagnosis of the child, treating the child as the diagnosis says.
- P54: Bowen estimated projection from the siblings' life adjustments, which reads it off the outcome.

The main off-theory trap: blame. The mother is "not malicious; she is just anxious" (P22), and the child plays an active part (P1, P36). A second trap: treating a child's symptom, or a parent's worry about a real problem, as proof of projection by itself. The sources contrast anxious focus with focus governed by the child's "reality needs" (P43, P44).

## 2. What the original authors wrote

### 2a. What projection is

#### Bowen

- <a id="P1"></a>**P1** “The term "family projection process" refers to the mechanisms that operate as the parents and the child play active parts in the transmission of the parental problem to the child.” Bowen, FTiCP ch8 L43. Both parents and child are active.
- <a id="P73"></a>**P73** “Impairment of one or more children. This is the pattern in which parents operate as a "we-ness" to project their undifferentiation to one or more children.” Bowen, FTiCP ch10 L179. Both parents, acting as one.
- <a id="P2"></a>**P2** “Family projection process. This is the basic process by which parental problems are projected to children. It is present in the full range of problems from the mildest to the most severe” Bowen, FTiCP ch10 L181.
- <a id="P3"></a>**P3** “The basic pattern involves a mother whose emotional system is more focused on children than on her husband and a father who is sensitive to his wife's anxiety and who supports her emotional involvement with the children.” Bowen, FTiCP ch10 L181. The father has a part too.
- <a id="P4"></a>**P4** “The child-focused family is one in which sufficient family anxiety is focused on one or more children to result in serious impairment in a child. The child focused energy is deeply imbedded, and it includes the full range of emotional involvements from the most positive to the most negative.” Bowen, FTiCP ch14 L51.
- <a id="P5"></a>**P5** “It results in primary emotional impairment of the child; or, it can superimpose itself on some defect or on some chronic physical illness or disability. It exists in all gradations of intensity” Bowen, FTiCP ch16 L177. A real defect does not rule projection out.
- <a id="P6"></a>**P6** “The most frequent pattern is one which operates through the mother with the mechanism which enables the mother to become less anxious by focusing on the child.” Bowen, FTiCP ch21 L45.
- <a id="P7"></a>**P7** “The family deals with its problem, its immaturities, by projecting it forward into the next generation.” Bowen, BS2 L1572.

#### Kerr

- <a id="P8"></a>**P8** “In another equally well differentiated family, when the parents get more anxious, they may get more preoccupied with what is "wrong" with one of their children. This child may become sufficiently anxious himself that he develops a symptom (physical, emotional, or social).” Kerr, FE7 L17. The parents' anxiety, the focus, and the child's symptom are three separate steps.
- <a id="P9"></a>**P9** “The word "projection" implies a psychological process. While psychological processes are important in the transmission of parental anxieties and immaturities, the deeper (phylogenetically older) emotional attachment between mother and child is the most basic component of the transmission process.” Kerr, FE7 L301 (footnote 22). Not projection in the psychoanalytic sense.
- <a id="P10"></a>**P10** “A well entrenched symbiotic relationship, therefore, is most accurately conceptualized as a mutual projection process.” Kerr, FE7 L301 (footnote 22).

#### Havstad

- <a id="P11"></a>**P11** “patterns that shift it include reciprocal relationships, triangles, cutoff, conflict, emotional distance, and transfers of anxiety to weaker members.” Havstad and Sheffield, ART L46. Her phrase for it: transfers of anxiety to weaker members.

#### Patrick (writing and rulings)

- <a id="P13"></a>**P13** “Conflict, reciprocity, and triangle/projection allow one to maintain toward moves despite the tension. Equilibrium is an absence of mechanisms.” Patrick, EEM L21 (2021). His model files projection with the toward moves.
- <a id="P12"></a>**P12** “Value space: conflict, distance, reciprocal functioning, child-focus, cutoff, inside (triangle), outside (triangle), defined-self.” Patrick, WP L51 (2025).

### 2b. How it shows: the steps

#### Bowen

- <a id="P14"></a>**P14** “There are three main steps in each episode in the projection process. ... The first is a feeling-thinking step. It begins with a feeling in the mother which merges into thinking about defects in the child. The second is the examining-labeling step in which she searches for and diagnoses a defect in the child that best fits her feeling state.” Bowen, FTiCP ch8 L47.
- <a id="P15"></a>**P15** “The third step is the treating step in which she acts toward and treats the child as though her diagnosis is accurate. The projection system can create its own defects.” Bowen, FTiCP ch8 L47.
- <a id="P16"></a>**P16** “The projection is fed by the mother's anxiety. When the cause for her anxiety is located outside of the mother, the anxiety subsides. For the child, accepting the projection as a reality is a small price to pay for a calmer mother.” Bowen, FTiCP ch8 L47.
- <a id="P17"></a>**P17** “One need listen to such a mother only a few minutes to hear her invoke outside opinions, diagnoses, and tests to validate the projection.” Bowen, FTiCP ch8 L49. What a coder may hear: a string of diagnoses of the child.
- <a id="P18"></a>**P18** “For instance, a mother in her calmer periods can know that nagging makes the child's problem worse. She may resolve to stop the nagging, only to have it recur automatically when anxiety rises.” Bowen, FTiCP ch14 L51.

#### Kerr

- <a id="P20"></a>**P20** “Parents often describe differences in the relationships with their various children in terms of how much energy or worry is tied up in a particular child.” Kerr, FE7 L183.
- <a id="P21"></a>**P21** “The second step is for mother to assign meaning or significance to her perceptions of the child. This interpretation is based on her feeling state. Her image of the child reflects her anxiety and emotional needs ... more than it reflects the child himself.” Kerr, FE7 L187.
- <a id="P22"></a>**P22** “The final step is when the child internalizes mother's image of him and the circle is completed. ... The mother is not malicious; she is just anxious. She is as much a prisoner of the situation as the child.” Kerr, FE7 L187.
- <a id="P23"></a>**P23** “Parents and other family members "do in" the child by continually "diagnosing" him as "incompetent" or "inadequate." The parent is "sure" he or she is "right" and the child yields to the "fact" that the parent "knows best."” Kerr, FE7 L225.
- <a id="P24"></a>**P24** “Other parent-child relationship patterns foster an outcome of a child's growing up to be highly reactive to his parents' problems and their need for emotional support. ... they adopt an overly helpful rather than a helpless stance in the family.” Kerr, FE7 L227. The focus can produce a helpful child as well as a helpless one (see [`reciprocity.md`](reciprocity.md), RF85).

### 2c. Which child

#### Bowen

- <a id="P72"></a>**P72** “Most mothers work hard to treat all their children the same, but the average mother has one child with whom the fusion is far more intense. The intensely fused child may be one who has a positive attachment to the mother, one who was considered strange or different from infancy, or one whom the mother repulsed from birth-and vice versa.” Bowen, FTiCP ch10 L183. Attachment, oddness and rejection all qualify.
- <a id="P25"></a>**P25** “Among the most vulnerable for the projection process are oldest children, the oldest boy or oldest girl, an only boy or only girl, a child born when anxiety is high, and a child born with a defect.” Bowen, FTiCP ch10 L187.
- <a id="P26"></a>**P26** “It focuses first on one child. If the amount is too great for that child, the process will select others for lesser degrees of involvement.” Bowen, FTiCP ch16 L181.

#### Kerr

- <a id="P29"></a>**P29** “Family stories about whether a child was "wanted" or "unwanted" have little to do with why a particular child becomes the main target of parental anxiety and undifferentiation.” Kerr, FE7 L231.

### 2d. Who starts it? (open; status item 2)

Each claim below is a hypothesis about order. Record the dates of the parent's anxiety, the child's problem and the focus separately; the order is then read from the dates.

#### Bowen

- <a id="P75"></a>**P75** “The mothers' efforts to change the patients are timed with anxieties in themselves, and not with the reality situation in the patients.” Bowen, FTiCP ch4 L74. Supports the parent's-anxiety-first reading (OPEN_QUESTIONS 3, still open). From the NIMH families.
- <a id="P31"></a>**P31** “The parents may force the projection against resistance until the child finally accepts the projection, but most often the parents initiate the projection and the child accepts it. Or the child may initiate the projection and coerce the parents to agree that he is the cause of the family problem.” Bowen, FTiCP ch8 L43. Either side can start it.
- <a id="P32"></a>**P32** “The process begins with anxiety in the mother. The child responds anxiously to mother, which she misperceives as a problem in the child.” Bowen, FTiCP ch16 L185. The basis for option (b).
- <a id="P33"></a>**P33** “Once the process has started, it can be motivated either by anxiety in the mother, or anxiety in the child.” Bowen, FTiCP ch16 L185. Same paragraph as P32.
- <a id="P34"></a>**P34** “Specifically, it begins with an overanxious mother devoted to being the best possible mother and having the most wonderful child. The child becomes anxious in response to the mother's anxiety.” Bowen, FTiCP ch18 L92.
- <a id="P19"></a>**P19** “the family projection process is motivated as I see it by mostly by anxiety in the mother. and when the mother is anxious she does more worrying about the child and when she's less anxious she does less worrying.” Bowen, BS2 L2765. "mostly", not always.
- <a id="P35"></a>**P35** “the mother's worry system can create a defect here out of old cloth in which there is none” Bowen, BS2 L2973. A problem the parent's worry made, not one the child had first.
- <a id="P36"></a>**P36** “I don't like scapegoating because it implies that it's something that parents do to the child. To me, it's more profitable to think of that as a complementary thing in which both the child and the parents play a part.” Bowen, BS3 L53.

#### Kerr

- <a id="P37"></a>**P37** “Distress that begins in the mother about some event in her personal life may be first reacted to by her most undifferentiated child, perhaps her daughter. As the child becomes distressed, her behavior may be affected in a way that troubles the mother.” Kerr, FE5 L85. The mother's own event comes first, then the child's reaction.
- <a id="P38"></a>**P38** “This adds to the mother's anxiety, which may then shift off that which distressed her initially and focus on the child's emerging problems.” Kerr, FE5 L85. Read with P37, the order is the mother's own distress, the child's reaction, more anxiety in the mother, then the focus (Claude's reading).
- <a id="P39"></a>**P39** “So the process that ultimately determines how much a child grows away from his family begins in the family and not in the child. However, while the process begins in the family (the mother having the most direct influence on the child), it is soon reinforced by the child.” Kerr, FE7 L177.
- <a id="P40"></a>**P40** “The pattern "begins" (once the pattern is entrenched it can be activated by either mother or child) when anxiety develops in the mother related to some aspect of her child's functioning. She may react to something the child actually said or did, to something she imagined the child said or did, or to something she fears the child might say or do.” Kerr, FE7 L187. "Begins" is in Kerr's own quotation marks.
- <a id="P41"></a>**P41** “The process of parents' focusing anxiously on a child can be set in motion by increased anxiety in the child as well as by increased anxiety in the parents. Who gets anxious first is less important than the process of one person's anxiety infecting another and a vicious circle ensuing.” Kerr, FE7 L281 (footnote 2). The basis for option (c).

### 2e. What else it might be

#### Bowen

- <a id="P76"></a>**P76** “We have not had a rejecting mother in our small number of research families.” Bowen, FTiCP ch4 L116. Attention that falls when the mother's anxiety falls is felt as rejection; it is not coded as rejection. Sample: up to 14 research families.
- <a id="P42"></a>**P42** “The fact that serious impairment of children can develop in calm, harmonious marriages is further evidence that impairment of children can occur without conflict.” Bowen, FTiCP ch9 L69. No marital conflict is needed.
- <a id="P27"></a>**P27** “On a simplistic level, it is related to the degree of emotional turn on or turn off (both equal in emotional systems terms) the mother feels for the child. This is an automatic emotional process that is not changed by acting the opposite.” Bowen, FTiCP ch16 L181. Warmth and rejection count the same.
- <a id="P43"></a>**P43** “The anxious parental effort goes into sympathetic, solicitous, overprotective energy, which is directed more by the mother's anxiety than the reality needs of the child.” Bowen, FTiCP ch16 L185.

#### Kerr

- <a id="P48"></a>**P48** “Another common example of fairly inflexible functioning positions is a triangle of two parents and a child in which the child functions as an emotional appendage of his parents. He chronically adapts his thoughts, feelings, and behavior to reduce tension in the parents.” Kerr, FE6 L53. The same focus described as a triangle; which code applies is open ([`reciprocity.md`](reciprocity.md) status item 11 raises the same choice).
- <a id="P46"></a>**P46** “Blaming one person for the lack of emotional separation between a child (or young adult) and his family is like crediting one musician for the sounds of an entire orchestra.” Kerr, FE7 L177.
- <a id="P45"></a>**P45** “Parents can also exaggerate a trait, a feeling, or a type of functioning that is inherent in the child by anxiously focusing on it.” Kerr, FE7 L191. A real trait in the child and projection can coexist.
- <a id="P44"></a>**P44** “In summary, the greater the degree of differentiation between mother and child, the more their relationship will be governed by the reality needs of the child.” Kerr, FE7 L229. Focus on a real need is the contrast case.
- <a id="P47"></a>**P47** “The "patient" is no more the cause of the family's distress than the family is the cause of the "patient's" distress.” Kerr, FE9 L31.

### 2f. Where the anxiety goes

#### Bowen

- <a id="P49"></a>**P49** “The family projection process is universal in that it exists in all families to some degree. It alleviates the anxiety of undifferentiation in the present generation at the expense of the next generation.” Bowen, FTiCP ch10 L189.
- <a id="P50"></a>**P50** “It is as if there is a definite amount of undifferentiation to be absorbed by marital conflict, sickness in a spouse, and projection to the children. The amount absorbed in conflict or sickness in a spouse reduces the amount that will be directed to the children.” Bowen, FTiCP ch16 L179. An "as if" model, not a measured one.

#### Kerr

- <a id="P52"></a>**P52** “When parental anxiety is bound through focus on a child, the marital relationship is less likely to be disturbed.” Kerr, FE7 L17.
- <a id="P30"></a>**P30** “A family emotional system is programmed (based on emotional process of preceding generations) to focus its anxiety on certain places in the system. Anxiety that is not bound in the parental generation will usually focus on a child.” Kerr, FE7 L231.

### 2g. Evidence (open; status item 7)

#### Bowen

- <a id="P55"></a>**P55** “I have never seen a family in which children were equally involved in the family emotional process.” Bowen, FTiCP ch16 L181.
- <a id="P54"></a>**P54** “In doing multigenerational family histories, it is relatively easy to estimate the family projection process and identify the triangled child by securing historical data about the life adjustments of each sibling.” Bowen, FTiCP ch16 L187. The estimate comes from outcomes, not from dated moves.

#### Kerr

- <a id="P56"></a>**P56** “Family myths about who was a "favorite" or who was an "outsider" are also notoriously unreliable indicators of what actually transpired between a child and his family.” Kerr, FE7 L231.

## 4. Worked examples

The questions stay open where the rulings leave them open.

### 4a. Arthur: a colicky sister and a mother "on the verge of fraying" (IRR synthetic case)

#### Coders' group (IRR)

- <a id="P66"></a>**P66** “When Claire was born, that shift became permanent. She was a colicky baby, always crying. My mother seemed constantly on the verge of fraying.” Arthur's statement 2192, IRR M5 @00:56:28.
- <a id="P67"></a>**P67** “If things got too loud with Clara, he'd just find a reason to leave the room.” Arthur's statement 2194, IRR M6 @00:18:11.

#### Questions

The child's problem (colic) is dated to her birth and comes before the mother's "fraying". Under option (a) this alone qualifies; under (b) and (c) it qualifies too (status item 2). Is a colicky baby a perceived problem or a real need (P44)? Nothing in 2192 says the mother focused on Claire; the focus is theory's expectation, so it is a labelled hypothesis (status item 3). The father's leaving the room is the stated move, and Guillermo read it as the father's side of a child-focused triangle (P58).

### 4b. Bowen's consultation family: the focus moves from child to child

#### Bowen

- <a id="P68"></a>**P68** “You can take out the object of the family projection process and then a certain amount of that will pick up the next child or the next vulnerable child.” Bowen, BS3 L382.
- <a id="P69"></a>**P69** “Okay so here was the second child and he was about I'd say seven. And he was in a hospital, some kind of a chronic, I think it was a heart thing if I'm mistaken. So he was out. And then at that time this next child who was about six began to pick up symptoms.” Bowen, BS3 L384.

#### Questions

The order is given (oldest institutionalised, then the second child's heart problem worsens, then the third child's symptoms) with ages only. The second child had a physical defect; projection can "superimpose itself on some defect" (P5). Each symptom is a dated fact; the projection that links them is Bowen's reading.

### 4c. Bowen's family in psychotherapy: parental conflict, then projection again

#### Bowen

- <a id="P51"></a>**P51** “About three months after he started, there was a period of overt conflict between the parents. The parents quickly joined forces and began another period of projection of the family problem to the son. The parental conflict subsided and the son went into another period of regression” Bowen, FTiCP ch8 L113.

#### Questions

Conflict between the parents, then focus on the son, then the son's regression, with one interval ("about three months"). Is the parents' "joining forces" a dated relationship move of its own? It fits P50: what goes to conflict does not go to the child.


## 5. Readings that look right but are off-theory

| Reading | What the sources say instead | Entries |
|---|---|---|
| The mother causes the child's problem (bad mothering, scapegoating) | Both parents and the child play active parts; the mother "is not malicious; she is just anxious" | P1, P36, P22, P46 |
| Projection is a psychological defence, as in psychoanalysis | Kerr: the deeper component is the emotional attachment; "a mutual projection process" | P9, P10 |
| It always starts with the parent's anxiety | Bowen and Kerr both say either side can start or drive it once it is established | P31, P33, P40, P41 |
| It always starts with a problem in the child | Bowen: the worry can make a defect "out of old cloth"; Patrick ruled this out as the only case | P35, P32 |
| A favoured child is spared | "turn on or turn off (both equal)"; the focus runs "from the most positive to the most negative" | P27, P4 |
| A child's symptom proves projection | Bowen's estimate reads the process off outcomes; a real defect or need can draw real concern | P54, P44, P5 |
| Worry about a real problem is not projection | A real defect or trait can be exaggerated by anxious focus; the test is whether the focus follows the parent's anxiety or the child's reality needs | P5, P45, P43 |
| Projection needs marital conflict | Impairment of children develops in "calm, harmonious marriages" | P42 |
| Family stories show which child was focused on | Stories of "wanted", "favorite" or "outsider" are unreliable | P29, P56 |
| Ask the parent whether they project | It is coded without asking; it is too automatic to know (status item 1) | R-0435 |
