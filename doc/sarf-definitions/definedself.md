# Defined self (DS)

For coders: what the original authors wrote about defining a self (the "I position"), where to find it, and what is decided. Read it for food for thought while coding. It is not a rule book; section 1 says which rules exist.

- Built 2026-09-26 from the sources listed in [`README.md`](README.md). Every quote below was checked word for word against its source file by the quote checker; the check is repeatable.
- This is the public edition: excerpts from confidential sources are left out, and so are the paths to the source files ([`README.md`](README.md)).
- Ids (DS1, DS2, ...) are stable. Location keys (FE4 L106, BS4 L687, BKIS-12-pt1 @00:20:20, ...) are explained in [`README.md`](README.md).
- Transcripts of recordings are machine transcriptions. A word marked [sic] is as transcribed; "eye position" is the transcriber's spelling of "I position".
- Bowen's, Kerr's and Havstad's statements are ideas. None was tested with counted data unless the note says so.

## Coder summary

Nothing about this code is settled for coding. What kind of thing a defined self is (a relationship move, a functioning shift, or a third kind) is open for the IRR group (status item 5). Whether the others' "change back" reaction must be seen before it is coded is open too (status item 8). The only rulings are two picture rules and the rule that a reading from theory stays apart from the facts (status items 3, 4, 9).

The passages that matter most:
- DS13: Bowen, "A self is never defined in words. It's defined only when you can take an action stand."
- DS3 and DS4: Bowen's I position, "what I will do or will not do", without impinging on others; the "irresponsible I" makes demands.
- DS30: Kerr, "Many so-called "I positions" are really attempts to get others to change".
- DS43: Kerr's test, the "absolutely predictable" response "You are wrong; change back", without which the effort is "probably inconsequential".
- DS37 with DS42: Bowen's version of that test cannot fail as stated, and on tape he limits it himself ("accurate within certain limits").

The main off-theory trap: coding a defined self from the words of a statement ("I think", "I believe", "I won't") or from a firm demand ("go, or else"). Bowen and Kerr both say the words are not the self, and a demand on the other is the low-level kind. Record the act, its date, and any dated reaction by others; the label stays a reading until the IRR group rules (status items 5, 8, 9).

## 2. What the original authors wrote

### 2a. What a defined self is

#### Bowen

- <a id="DS1"></a>**DS1** “Of importance are the "action" stands which have to do with "what I will do and will not do."” Bowen, FTiCP ch9 L113. Said of the therapist defining himself to families; the stand is about the speaker's own conduct.
- <a id="DS2"></a>**DS2** “The basic self is not negotiable in the relationship system in that it is not changed by coercion or pressure, or to gain approval, or enhance one's stand with others.” Bowen, FTiCP ch21 L27.
- <a id="DS3"></a>**DS3** “The "I position" defines principle and action in terms of, "This is what I think, or believe" and, "This is what I will do or will not do," without impinging one's own values or beliefs on others.” Bowen, FTiCP ch21 L117. Principle and action together, and the test is "without impinging" on others.
- <a id="DS4"></a>**DS4** “The "responsible I" avoids the "irresponsible I" which makes demands on others with, "I want, or I deserve, or this is my right, or my privilege."” Bowen, FTiCP ch21 L117. A sentence that starts with "I" can be the irresponsible kind.
- <a id="DS5"></a>**DS5** “basically being a self means your beliefs, what do you stand for, what's important to you, and being able to state it, not in an antagonistic way, but, you know, firmly.” Bowen, BKIS-05-pt1b @00:07:25. Here stating the position does count; compare DS13.

#### Kerr

- <a id="DS6"></a>**DS6** “This process of change has been called "defining a self" because visible action is taken to which others respond. A change in basic level can be achieved while in relationship to emotionally significant others, but not when others are avoided or when one's actions disrupt a relationship.” Kerr, FE4 L104. Two parts a coder can look for: a visible action, and others responding to it.
- <a id="DS7"></a>**DS7** “An effort toward more differentiation does not insist others change and it is not contingent on anyone's cooperation. More importantly, it is not fueled by anger.” Kerr, FE4 L108.


#### Patrick (writing and rulings)

- <a id="DS9"></a>**DS9** “One person should not criticize another for that which is automatic in them. They should simply define and act out their own boundaries in relation to that which is automatic in another person.” Patrick, PiN L173 (2019 draft). His one written use of "boundaries" for it; see the off-theory table.
- <a id="DS10"></a>**DS10** “I saw a no-self position held by someone I had assumed was a leader. I also saw a move to define self in someone I assumed was a chronic no-self for the first time.” Patrick, LOG L65 (2021). From logging moves over a few minutes: he recorded it as a move at a moment.
- <a id="DS11"></a>**DS11** “Relationship shifts are automatic moves that a person makes in relation to one or more other people. "Automatic" means that it was driven by emotion, without choice” Patrick, WP L53 (2025). The definition that sits awkwardly with a chosen stand (status item 1).

### 2b. Words or action

#### Bowen

- <a id="DS121"></a>**DS121** “If a differentiating effort is to be successful, it has to take place in action, as a result of careful private planning, and without previous announcement of one's plan.” Bowen, FTiCP ch20 L1. An announced plan ("I'm setting a boundary") is words. The stand is the action.
- <a id="DS12"></a>**DS12** “I put this in sort of to convey a notion of what I mean by the I position and some of the magical things that can happen when one person can be a self.” Bowen, BS4 L683. The close of the tape, right after the parkway case (4c).
- <a id="DS13"></a>**DS13** “to be a self in an emotional system means you got to believe in it enough to take an action stand. A self is never defined in words. It's defined only when you can take an action stand.” Bowen, BS4 L685. The sentence the old review's "I think / I believe" marker contradicts.
- <a id="DS14"></a>**DS14** “It's just this is the ground I stand on, I believe in this, I'm going to stand here. I'm not going to, I give you the privilege of standing over there. I'm not going to hit you nor punish you.” Bowen, BS4 L690. The stand leaves the other free and carries no punishment.
- <a id="DS15"></a>**DS15** “if it's one that I have seen is people making are going home and proclaiming to be a self in words but actions sort of betray” Kerr or Bowen (speaker unclear), BKIS-05-pt1b @00:08:04. A self proclaimed in words, contradicted by what the person does.
- <a id="DS16"></a>**DS16** “I just like to go sit alongside the family and not react. And when any person can do that, the family can be calm.” Bowen, BKIS-06-pt2b @00:20:38.
- <a id="DS17"></a>**DS17** “I guess they pick this up in you in all kinds of ways. You don't have to spell it out. You don't have to spell it. You don't have to tell anybody about it. They know it.” Kerr and Bowen in exchange, BKIS-06-pt2b @00:21:01. Nothing said at all.

#### Kerr

- <a id="DS18"></a>**DS18** “Defining a self does not necessarily involve a strong statement of where one stands on a particular issue. A self is sometimes communicated most effectively by what is not said or done.” Kerr, FE6 L115.
- <a id="DS19"></a>**DS19** “Actions have more impact than words in a detriangling effort. ... Although he repeatedly tells family members about what is important to him and about where his viewpoints differ from theirs, their basic perception of him is amazingly unaltered.” Kerr, FE6 L127. Repeated statements of position changed nothing; actions did.
- <a id="DS20"></a>**DS20** “If a parent says "No" in reference to what he (the parent) will do ("I will not") rather than in reference to what the child "should" do ("You will not"), the child cannot use the parent to react off emotionally.” Kerr, FE7 L259. Kerr, like Bowen in the parkway case (4c), gives weight to the form of the sentence. The sentence still names the parent's own action.

### 2c. What looks like a defined self but is not

#### Bowen

- <a id="DS123"></a>**DS123** “The self-blamer is as irresponsible as the blamer in assuming responsibility for self.” Bowen, FTiCP ch8 L45. Self-blame is not a defined self.
- <a id="DS21"></a>**DS21** “If the individual attempts it without some conviction of his own, he is blindly following the advice of his therapist and is caught in a self-defeating togetherness with the therapist.” Bowen, FTiCP ch16 L139. A coached script is not a self.
- <a id="DS122"></a>**DS122** “The concept of differentiation has to do with self and not with others. Differentiation deals with working on one's own self, with controlling self, with becoming a more responsible person, and permitting others to be themselves.” Bowen, FTiCP ch17 L94. Winning, control or assertiveness over others is not a defined self.
- <a id="DS22"></a>**DS22** “I was almost as emotionally involved as ever, and I was using emotional distance and silence to create an illusion of nonresponsiveness. Distance and silence do not fool an emotional system.” Bowen, FTiCP ch21 L103. His own early stance with his family. Keeping quiet and away is not a stand.
- <a id="DS23"></a>**DS23** “As I see it, the rebel is responding to the emotional system. The rebel has an equal state of no self. ... He has no stand for himself. He's attacking the other.” Bowen, BS4 L454.
- <a id="DS24"></a>**DS24** “That kind of a posture is evidence of an attachment. And probably the more the individual has to say, I've worked it out, is evidence of an attachment.” Bowen, BKIS-06-pt2a @00:07:09. Said of people who announce they are independent of their parents.
- <a id="DS25"></a>**DS25** “From experience with this, I would say avoid confrontation. Confrontation probably says more about the immaturity of the accuser than it does about the personal deficit in the accused.” Bowen, BKIS-06-pt2a @00:22:22. Telling the family off is not defining a self.
- <a id="DS26"></a>**DS26** “But he stepped to his rule too rigidly and refused to take the wife and kids, and his newfound relatives refused to see him anymore.” Bowen, BKIS-06-pt2b @00:00:14. A trainee who was "pretty dogmatic in the application of it": a rule applied rigidly, followed by a cutoff by the relatives.
- <a id="DS27"></a>**DS27** “the low level self can have an eye position that is a selfish dogmatic forceful kind of an eye position that's a low level self and by the time you get on up to a high level self a high level differentiated self is neither offensive nor defensive to the other” Bowen, BKIS-12-pt1 @00:20:20. "eye position" [sic]. An I position exists at every level; how it is done tells the level.
- <a id="DS28"></a>**DS28** “in other words the poorly differentiated person says if you don't change I'm going to hit you in the head if you don't change in other words it is a threat” Bowen, BKIS-12-pt1 @00:21:49. A demand backed by a threat is the low-level kind.

#### Kerr

- <a id="DS29"></a>**DS29** “The "rugged individualist" operates as much in reaction to others as the compliant person.” Kerr, FE3 L185 (footnote 3).
- <a id="DS30"></a>**DS30** “Many so-called "I positions" are really attempts to get others to change or are attempts to pry oneself loose from emotionally intense situations. Some pronouncements, such as, "I am not going to get into your problem," are mostly efforts to avoid responsibility.” Kerr, FE4 L108. An I-statement that is an exit or a lever on the other.
- <a id="DS31"></a>**DS31** “When someone angrily and dogmatically claims to be a "self," he is usually unsure of his position and is blaming others for his plight in life.” Kerr, FE4 L108.
- <a id="DS32"></a>**DS32** “the ability to be in emotional contact with a difficult, emotionally charged problem and not feel compelled to preach about what others "should" do, not rush in to "fix" the problem, and not pretend to be detached by emotionally insulating oneself.” Kerr, FE4 L108. Three counterfeits in one sentence: preaching, fixing, insulating.
- <a id="DS33"></a>**DS33** “Functioning based on principle requires a tolerance of anxiety and a willingness to focus on self. Functioning based on feelings and subjectivity succumbs to the pressure for a quick reduction in anxiety and is aimed at changing others rather than changing self.” Kerr, FE5 L125.
- <a id="DS34"></a>**DS34** “Declaring one's "independence" from family is not differentiation of self. It does not resolve one's emotional fusion with the past. Most people who claim to be "independent" of their families have "broken away" from them rather than "grown away" from them.” Kerr, FE9 L82. A break or a cutoff is not a defined self.

#### Patrick (writing and rulings)

- <a id="DS35"></a>**DS35** “An amicable resignation from a board position is several simultaneous away moves from the resigner to each of the other board members ... So is avoiding a Christmas holiday.” Patrick, LOG L21 (2021). Skipping a family holiday is an away move in his own worked examples.

### 2d. The others' reaction: "change back"

#### Bowen

- <a id="DS120"></a>**DS120** “If he reaches the point of defining himself as the kind of husband and father he responsibly wants to be, and he makes a move in that direction, he meets immediate emotional opposition ... Increasing individuation is slow and difficult and it takes place only with a disciplined decision to stay on a principled course in spite of the urge to return to the togetherness.” Bowen, FTiCP ch13 L37. Bowen calls it "a move", and a chosen one; compare the white paper's "without choice" (DS11, status item 1).
- <a id="DS36"></a>**DS36** “The togetherness forces are so strong in maintaining the status quo that any small step toward differentiation is met with vigorous disapproval of the group.” Bowen, FTiCP ch16 L139.
- <a id="DS37"></a>**DS37** “any small step toward differentiation is accompanied by a small emotional upheaval in the family system. This pattern is so predictable that absence of an emotional reaction is good evidence that the differentiating effort was not successful.” Bowen, FTiCP ch21 L119. As stated, a reaction confirms the claim and no reaction confirms it too; it cannot fail.
- <a id="DS38"></a>**DS38** “There are three predictable steps in the family reaction to differentiation. They are: (1) "You are wrong," or some version of that; (2) "Change back," which can be communicated in many different ways; and (3) "If you do not, these are the consequences."” Bowen, FTiCP ch21 L119.
- <a id="DS39"></a>**DS39** “If the differentiating one can stay on course without defending self or counterattacking, the emotional reaction is usually brief and the other then expresses appreciation.” Bowen, FTiCP ch21 L119.
- <a id="DS40"></a>**DS40** “So anytime anybody makes a significant step toward differentiating a self, the rest of the emotional system attacks in these three steps.” Bowen, BS4 L271.
- <a id="DS41"></a>**DS41** “And this is so much a part of every emotional system that involves us all, that unless it happens, then something is, something is wrong.” Bowen, BS4 L299. The tape's version of DS37.
- <a id="DS42"></a>**DS42** “privately when I made a solid move the opposition would be throwing bricks so I would use the brick throwing as indication that I made a differentiating move which is accurate within certain limits.” Bowen, BKIS-12-pt2 @00:14:47. Bowen himself limits the reaction test.

#### Kerr

- <a id="DS43"></a>**DS43** “When someone attempts to be more of a self in a relationship system, the absolutely predictable response from important others is, "You are wrong; change back; if you don't, these are the consequences!" In fact, if such responses do not occur, one's efforts to define more of a self are probably inconsequential.” Kerr, FE4 L106. Kerr's test: the reaction of others, which a coder can date.
- <a id="DS44"></a>**DS44** “This is anxiety associated with trying to become more of a self, an anxiety of progression rather than regression.” Kerr, FE5 L113. The person's own anxiety can rise with the step. See the anxiety page, A53.
- <a id="DS45"></a>**DS45** “When one person in a family can function as a little more of a self, other family members automatically oppose and test it. Progress disturbs the togetherness as much as regression does.” Kerr, FE5 L138 (footnote 11).
- <a id="DS46"></a>**DS46** “There can be symptoms in another family member associated with someone's effort to change, but these symptoms will disappear when the system equilibrates on a higher level of functioning. If the symptoms do not disappear, this indicates that emotional distance has been a major component of the effort to "change."” Kerr, FE5 L138 (footnote 11). A second test, decided by what happens later.


#### Patrick (writing and rulings)

- <a id="DS48"></a>**DS48** “FACT: Taking a stand in important triangles can produce a predictable change-back move.” Patrick, VF L73 (2020). He wrote "can", not "does".

### 2f. Basic self, pseudo-self and functioning

#### Bowen

- <a id="DS56"></a>**DS56** “a good actor can appear so much for real that it can be difficult for the actor or for others without detailed knowledge of how emotional systems function to know the dividing line between solid self and pseudo-self.” Bowen, FTiCP ch16 L123. A single statement will not show which it is.
- <a id="DS57"></a>**DS57** “It is difficult to assess differentiation during calm periods in a life. ... The real test of the stability of differentiation comes when the person is again subjected to chronic severe stress.” Bowen, FTiCP ch16 L139.
- <a id="DS58"></a>**DS58** “The pseudo-self can accept a plausible sounding philosophy under the emotional influence of the moment, or it can just as easily adopt an opposite philosophy to oppose the relationship system.” Bowen, FTiCP ch21 L27. A stated belief, or its opposite, can be pseudo-self.
- <a id="DS59"></a>**DS59** “There is so much borrowing and trading of pseudo-self among those in the lower half of the scale that definite scale values can be estimated only from observations that cover months or years, or from a lifetime pattern.” Bowen, FTiCP ch21 L27. One move says nothing about a person's level.

#### Kerr

- <a id="DS60"></a>**DS60** “People can function at levels that are higher or lower than their basic level depending on the circumstances of the relationship system in which they are operating.” Kerr, FE4 L58. The paragraph goes on: one spouse's functioning can rise by "borrowing" self from the other. Functioning up is not by itself a defined self (status item 5).
- <a id="DS61"></a>**DS61** “Solid self is not negotiable in any relationship system and so little "borrowing" and "trading" of "self" occurs in well differentiated relationships.” Kerr, FE4 L82.

## 4. Worked examples

### 4a. Bowen's own family, the 1967 visit (FTiCP ch21)

Published by Bowen, first anonymously (case card bowen-own-family). Bowen is the only informant and also ran the effort.

#### Bowen

- <a id="DS87"></a>**DS87** “I ended the letter by saying that I would be home on a specific date, but since I had already said all that was necessary in the letter, it would not be necessary to see him unless he had something to say to me. I signed it, "Your Meddlesome Brother."” Bowen, FTiCP ch21 L179.
- <a id="DS88"></a>**DS88** “From long experience, I have found that a differentiating effort routinely fails if anyone else knows anything about it.” Bowen, FTiCP ch21 L183. By his own rule no one else could confirm the effort at the time.
- <a id="DS89"></a>**DS89** “My brother had reacted most severely to a "story" about him and threatened a libel suit against me.” Bowen, FTiCP ch21 L185. A dated reaction: the "consequences" step, in words.
- <a id="DS90"></a>**DS90** “About two-thirds through the meeting I knew I had been successful for I noted that the family system had lost its emotional punch” Bowen, FTiCP ch21 L187. Success judged by the person making the effort.
- <a id="DS91"></a>**DS91** “Two weeks after the visit I received a long letter from my mother in which she included one concise paragraph about the visit. In it she said, "With all its ups and downs, your last trip home was the greatest ever."” Bowen, FTiCP ch21 L191.
- <a id="DS92"></a>**DS92** “In the almost three-year interim since the family experience, the family has been on the best over-all level of adaptation in many years.” Bowen, FTiCP ch21 L193.
- <a id="DS93"></a>**DS93** “which was the thing that elated me the most it's not that people have asked how did you help your family or did your family like it the family didn't have anything to do with it it was just my own effort” Bowen, BKIS-05-pt1a @00:07:33. On tape, years later: the goal was his own conduct, not the family's response.

#### Questions

Which act is the defined self: the letters (after about 8 weeks of planning), the visit, or not being drawn in during the meeting? The letters are the only acts others could see. The brother's threat is a dated reaction, reported only by Bowen. Success is Bowen's own judgement (DS90), with no other observer by design (DS88). The later calm is the author's word ("adaptation"), with no counts, and shows nearness in time only (status item 9).

### 4b. The engaged young woman and her sister's letter (Basic Series 4)

#### Bowen

- <a id="DS94"></a>**DS94** “After the visit, she had a reading from an older sister who was the family spokesman in her family. The reading went, the letter went, you're wrong, even to the point of saying this is morally wrong” Bowen, BS4 L284.
- <a id="DS95"></a>**DS95** “Then the next step, change back, went, give him up. You'll never notice it in the future. You'll be over it in a short time.” Bowen, BS4 L287.
- <a id="DS96"></a>**DS96** “And the third step was, if you don't, then I'm not going to have anything to do with you in the future, and your brothers will not be your brothers anymore.” Bowen, BS4 L290.

#### Questions

The defining act is going ahead with the marriage, an action, not a statement. The three steps arrive in one letter, which is datable. The family's withdrawal lasts "a few months". The better outcome ("much closer to the family") is Bowen's report, and he told the story to show the steps.

### 4c. The parkway: a change of words (Basic Series 4)

#### Bowen

- <a id="DS98"></a>**DS98** “So as they approached the turnoff for the parkway, she said very nicely, honey, would you please turn and let's go up the parkway. His reaction was a vigorous blow up” Bowen, BS4 L641.

#### Questions

Is "I do not like to ride through that busy business district" a defined self? It is an I-statement, and Bowen calls it "the magic of the I position". It is a change of wording coached by the therapist (compare DS21), it asks nothing of the husband, and no change-back reaction followed. Kerr's test (DS43) would call it "probably inconsequential". Thirty lines later Bowen says "A self is never defined in words" (DS13). No ruling settles which applies.

### 4g. Ms. D's family vacation (Harrison 2013)

A published case with fictional names (case card harrison-anna).

#### Other

- <a id="DS109"></a>**DS109** “This time, she instead offered to … watch the children on only one of the five days.” Harrison, HA13 L250. "…" skips the other column of the page.
- <a id="DS110"></a>**DS110** “admitted her inability to help and encouraged her mother … to talk to older and wiser members of the family.” Harrison, HA13 L256.
