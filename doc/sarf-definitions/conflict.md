For coders: what the original authors wrote about conflict, where to find it, and what is decided. Read it for food for thought while coding. It is not a rule book; section 1 says which rules exist.

- Transcripts of recordings are machine transcriptions. A word marked [sic] is as transcribed.

## Coder summary

Conflict is one of the named relationship moves in the white paper's list (status item 4). Two rules exist. A reported argument is one conflict event with a mover and a target (R-0434, status item 2). Conflict is its own two-person move, never a triangle position (R-0288, status item 3). Everything else is open for the IRR group. That includes the longest-running dispute, whether a silent standoff is conflict or distance (status item 10). It also includes whether an argument counts as a toward or an away move (status item 5).

The passages that matter most:
- C1: Bowen's definition, each spouse "fights for an equal share of the common self and neither gives in to the other".
- C23: Kerr, conflict gives emotional contact and emotional distance at the same time.
- C6: each side is "simultaneously fighting against the other's attempts to control"; no one person is the cause.
- C14: the issue fought over is not the cause of the conflict. Code the move, not the topic.
- C54, C58: Havstad in the IRR meeting, "There can be a Cold War"; digging in one's heels quietly is not the same as distancing.

The main off-theory trap: coding the content of a fight (money, the children, a third person) or blaming one side, instead of recording the dated move. A second trap: reading a quiet stretch as no conflict (C15, C16).

## 1. Status of the code

### Patrick (writing and rulings)

1. **Record a shift, never a level.** RULED. Ruling R-0054 (records shifts, not levels) — see the private edition. A marriage that is "always fighting" goes in once as a baseline, not as a new event each time (CODING section 8).
2. **One argument is one conflict event, with a mover and a target.** RULED. Ruling R-0434 (one argument is one conflict event, with a mover and a target) — see the private edition. Bowen and Kerr describe conflict as mutual (C1, C6). How a one-mover record carries the other side is not ruled (Claude's reading).
3. **Conflict is its own move, not a triangle position.** RULED. Ruling R-0288 (conflict is its own move, not a triangle position) — see the private edition. Kerr's triangle puts the conflict on whichever side carries it (C27, C28).
4. **Conflict is one kind in the value space; relationship moves are automatic.** Patrick's white paper, his writing and not a ruling: C8, C9. Whether "automatic ... without choice" fits every conflict is not discussed in the sources.
5. **Is an argument a toward move or an away move?** OPEN (OPEN_QUESTIONS 14). By contact it is two toward moves (C10, C11). By desirability it is an away. Tone is a separate axis (C12). Who settles it: the IRR group.
6. **Coding questions stay open for the IRR group, including arguments about a third person.** RULED. Ruling R-0439 (coding judgment calls are left to the IRR group) — see the private edition. Meanwhile the coach's prompt carries a best-guess rule marked provisional (R-0440).
7. **Every coding rule here is provisional.** Ruling R-0511 (coding rules are provisional while ground truth is rebuilt) — see the private edition.
8. **A conflict code does not imply an anxiety shift.** RULED 2026-09-26. An anxiety shift needs its own evidence ([`anxiety.md`](anxiety.md) status item 6). Kerr's claim that conflict binds anxiety (C35) is theory, not a coding rule.
9. **A reading from theory stays apart from the facts.** RULED 2026-09-26. See the private corpus for the app's decision on this point (2026-09-26). (OPEN_QUESTIONS 5). So father–daughter fighting is coded as conflict between them; Kerr's reading that it is "acted out" marital tension (C28) is kept, if at all, as a labelled hypothesis.

### Coders' group (IRR)

10. **Is a silent standoff conflict or distance?** OPEN, for the IRR group (OPEN_QUESTIONS 12; IRR results, unresolved item 2). Three readings, about 25 minutes in IRR meeting 4, no ruling: conflict (Havstad, C54, C58); distance from both sides (Patrick, C55); distance as the outcome of an unresolved conflict (Guillermo, C56). Section 3a has the discussion; worked example 4a is the statement.

## 2. What the original authors wrote

### 2a. What conflict is

#### Bowen

- <a id="C1"></a>**C1** “Marital conflict in which each spouse fights for an equal share of the common self and neither gives in to the other.” Bowen, FTiCP ch9 L69. The mark of conflict is that neither gives in.
- <a id="C3"></a>**C3** “Both may try for the dominant role, which results in conflict; or both may try for the adaptive role, which results in decision paralysis.” Bowen, FTiCP ch16 L165. Conflict and one-sided adapting are two outcomes of the same pressure.
- <a id="C2"></a>**C2** “The basic pattern in conflictual marriages is one in which neither gives in to the other or in which neither is capable of an adaptive role. These marriages are intense in the amount of emotional energy each invests in the other.” Bowen, FTiCP ch16 L169.
- <a id="C4"></a>**C4** “Marital conflict is sufficiently different that it can be almost in a category by itself. These are people who are emotionally focused on each other.” Bowen, BS5 L40.

#### Kerr

- <a id="C5"></a>**C5** “People continually pressure one another to think and do things their way, but this pressure is met on both sides with rebellion. As a consequence, the relationship is characterized by disharmony.” Kerr, FE3 L145. Pressure and rebellion on both sides.
- <a id="C6"></a>**C6** “While, to some extent, each person is attempting to control how the other thinks and acts, each is simultaneously fighting against the other's attempts to control or influence him.” Kerr, FE3 L147. No single mover causes a conflict; compare the one-mover record in status item 2.
- <a id="C7"></a>**C7** “A hallmark of a conflictual marriage is that husband and wife are angry and dissatisfied with one another. While the atmosphere of conflictual relationships is intensely negative much of the time, it is usually punctuated by periods of equally intense, sometimes very passionate, closeness.” Kerr, FE7 L121.
- <a id="C66"></a>**C66** “No matter how much they fight, or even how destructive the fighting sometimes is, conflictual mates are generally very "stuck" to one another. ... Each is apprehensive about the next ride, but each is aware of doing the very things that bring it about.” Kerr, FE7 L121. Both spouses do what brings the next fight; compare the one-mover record (status item 2) and two toward moves (status item 5).

#### Havstad

- <a id="C38"></a>**C38** “patterns that shift it include reciprocal relationships, triangles, cutoff, conflict, emotional distance, and transfers of anxiety to weaker members.” Havstad and Sheffield, ART L46. Conflict, distance and cutoff listed as separate patterns.


#### Patrick (writing and rulings)

- <a id="C8"></a>**C8** “Value space: conflict, distance, reciprocal functioning, child-focus, cutoff, inside (triangle), outside (triangle), defined-self.” Patrick, SARF white paper WP L51 (2025).
- <a id="C9"></a>**C9** “Relationship shifts are automatic moves that a person makes in relation to one or more other people. "Automatic" means that it was driven by emotion, without choice” Patrick, WP L53 (2025).
- <a id="C11"></a>**C11** “There are four ways to accomplish this. Distance is synonymous with away. Conflict, reciprocity, and triangle/projection allow one to maintain toward moves despite the tension.” Patrick, EEM L21 (2021).
- <a id="C12"></a>**C12** “A toward move would indicate that the person sees some value in engaging with that individual, regardless of whether a psychologist would perceive that as positive (BT: "triangling" / "reciprocity") or negative (BT: "conflict").” Patrick, CD L20 (2020). Conflict as a toward move with a negative tone.
- <a id="C10"></a>**C10** “A phone call is a toward move by one person to another. An argument is two simultaneous toward moves from each individual to the other.” Patrick, LOG L19 (2021). Two moves for one argument; R-0434 records one event (status item 2).

### 2b. How conflict shows, and what the fight is about

#### Bowen

- <a id="C18"></a>**C18** “The emotional investment in each other continues; only it shifts into negative energy that accuses, indicts, and diagnoses.” Bowen, FTiCP ch9 L117. Accusing and diagnosing the other are how conflict sounds.

#### Kerr

- <a id="C13"></a>**C13** “Efforts to get others to change can also escalate to problematic levels through cycles of each person's feeling criticized, getting defensive, and counterattacking. The flames are fanned by each blaming the other for the escalation.” Kerr, FE5 L71.
- <a id="C14"></a>**C14** “people do not have trouble getting along because of issues in these areas. These issues tend to bring out the emotional immaturity of people and it is that immaturity, not the issues, that creates the conflict.” Kerr, FE7 L125. The topic of a fight is a note, not the code.
- <a id="C15"></a>**C15** “While his wife's anger is usually overt, his anger and criticism of her are often expressed in covert, passive-aggressive ways.” Kerr, FE7 L128. One side of a conflict can be quiet. This bears on the silent standoff (status item 10).
- <a id="C17"></a>**C17** “Rebellion invites efforts to control and efforts to control invite rebellion.” Kerr, FE7 L130.
- <a id="C16"></a>**C16** “A lot of conflict is created by attempts to avoid conflict. After a long series of actions and inactions designed to avoid stirring things up, when conflict finally erupts, it is often much more polarized than it would have been had the issues been addressed earlier.” Kerr, FE7 L132. Avoiding can come before the fight; the eruption is the datable part.
- <a id="C37"></a>**C37** “in a conflictual marriage each spouse is convinced that it is the other spouse who needs to do the changing” Kerr, FE7 L123. A statement that the other must change is a candidate sign; it is not ruled as one.

### 2c. Conflict and distance at the same time

#### Bowen

- <a id="C20"></a>**C20** “And I would see marital conflict as a way of these people maintaining the distance from each other. ... They're uncomfortable with the distance, they get too close, that's uncomfortable, and then they fight and get the distance again.” Bowen, BS2 L1366. A fight is a way of getting distance.
- <a id="C22"></a>**C22** “In other words they get the most intense level of togetherness followed by separation, distance, coming back together. So it's an alternating relationship. Even in their most negative phases they are thinking about and focused on each other.” Bowen, BS5 L44.
- <a id="C21"></a>**C21** “The relationship cycles through periods of intense closeness, conflict that provides a period of emotional distance, and making up, which starts another cycle of intense closeness.” Bowen, FTiCP ch16 L169. A cycle, so one period can hold closeness, conflict and distance in turn.

#### Kerr

- <a id="C23"></a>**C23** “Emotionally based conflict provides a type of solution to the relationship dilemma. The focus on one another provides emotional contact, and the anger and stubborn refusals to do what the other wants provide emotional distance.” Kerr, FE3 L149. Contact and distance at once, which is why conflict is not simply an away move (status item 5).
- <a id="C24"></a>**C24** “A second way conflict can promote well-being is that the tension and arguments justify people's maintaining a comfortable distance from one another without feeling guilty about it.” Kerr, FE7 L147.
- <a id="C25"></a>**C25** “If person A expresses to person C that he is angry at person B , this communication functions to increase the level of togetherness between A and C . ... In addition, A's anger at B functions to maintain distance between A and B” Kerr, FE6 L49. One complaint about a third person can carry two moves: toward the listener, away from the one complained about. R-0439 keeps "arguments about a third person" open.

### 2d. Conflict in a triangle: where it sits and how it moves

#### Bowen

- <a id="C68"></a>**C68** “Emotional conflict between two family members could disappear with the simultaneous development of conflict between two other family members.” Bowen, FTiCP ch7 L13. Conflict can move between pairs. Date each pair's conflict separately.

#### Kerr

- <a id="C27"></a>**C27** “At moderate tension levels, the outcome of this shifting process in a triangle is one uncomfortable or conflictual relationship and two fairly comfortable ones. The discomfort or tension may shift from relationship to relationship” Kerr, FE6 L19.
- <a id="C28"></a>**C28** “The marital tension does not disappear, however; instead, it is acted out as conflict between father and daughter. So there are still two positive sides in the triangle and one negative side.” Kerr, FE6 L21. The conflict is coded where the fighting is; "acted out" marital tension is the theory's reading (status item 9).
- <a id="C29"></a>**C29** “This sibling conflict, while often explained on the basis of "sibling rivalry," is actually just one side of a triangle, the side where the triangle's tensions are usually acted out.” Kerr, FE6 L92.
- <a id="C30"></a>**C30** “Triangles that involve one or both spouse's parents can stir conflict in a marriage to the point that people reduce or completely cut off contact with that part of the family to restore harmony in the marriage.” Kerr, FE7 L134. Conflict in the marriage, then a cutoff from the in-laws: two moves, two codes (see [`cutoff.md`](cutoff.md)).

### 2e. What conflict does: claims

#### Bowen

- <a id="C67"></a>**C67** “their own effort to talk out the difference results in greater difference!” Bowen, FTiCP ch5 L33. Talking it out can widen a conflict. Code the moves, not the intention.
- <a id="C31"></a>**C31** “The fact that there are some families with intense marital conflict and no impairment of children is evidence that marital conflict does not, within itself, cause problems in children.” Bowen, FTiCP ch9 L69.
- <a id="C34"></a>**C34** “I believe the conflictual marriage is an enduring one because of the energy investment. The amount of thinking time that goes into the other is probably greater than calm marriages.” Bowen, FTiCP ch9 L117. "I believe", "probably": a clinical impression.
- <a id="C32"></a>**C32** “we say that marital conflict causes problems in kids. Don't believe it. Marital conflict is part of it when the spouses are using all four of these mechanisms.” Bowen, BS5 L336. His rebuttal has a qualifier.
- <a id="C33"></a>**C33** “There can be overt conflict going throughout the system, overt disharmony. And then when one person gets sick, impinged, the conflict settles down, and the illness sort of becomes solidified.” Bowen, BS2 L1490. A claimed order: symptom onset, then less conflict. Record the dates; the direction is a hypothesis.

#### Kerr

- <a id="C35"></a>**C35** “This externalization of anxiety provides some protection for each person from the development of clinical symptoms. When conflict prevails, it is the relationship that is symptomatic.” Kerr, FE3 L149. "The relationship is symptomatic" is Kerr's phrase; a symptom code still needs a person and the interference test ([`anxiety.md`](anxiety.md) status item 2).
- <a id="C36"></a>**C36** “Conflict does not necessarily bind all the anxiety and immaturity of two parents. Poorly differentiated people can be fairly conflictual in spite of making many adjustments in their individual functioning” Kerr, FE7 L149. Conflict can sit beside other patterns.

### 2f. Order and timing: claims, never defaults

[`anxiety.md`](anxiety.md) status item 8 applies here too: record the order of the dates, and keep any direction as a hypothesis.

#### Bowen

- <a id="C40"></a>**C40** “one family member will move forward towards some individuality and the other will oppose and if the individually oriented if the differentiating person can hold that ground in spite of the opposition of the other then the conflict goes away” Bowen, BKIS-12-pt1 @00:04:00. A step toward self can bring opposition first; less conflict comes later, if at all.

#### Kerr

- <a id="C41"></a>**C41** “Early in a marriage, for example, the predominant pattern may be of one spouse adapting more than the other to preserve marital harmony. ... Later in the marriage, however, the pattern may shift to one of marital conflict.” Kerr, FE7 L25. A shift into conflict can be a dated event in a long marriage.
- <a id="C42"></a>**C42** “some marriages are highly conflictual while the children are growing up but become calmer after the children leave home. Some marriages become much more conflictual after children leave.” Kerr, FE7 L134. The same event, opposite directions.

### 4b. The migraines and the "friend" (Kerr's conflictual couple, FE ch7)

A published case (case card kerr-migraines-friend).

#### Kerr

- <a id="C62"></a>**C62** “The couple entered therapy because of marked escalation in the level of conflict between them. They had been married nearly twenty years and had survived many stormy periods.” Kerr, FE7 L139.
- <a id="C63"></a>**C63** “"This has been the best two weeks of our married life," he said. ... "I have spent much of the last two weeks in and out of bed with migraine headaches," she said.” Kerr, FE7 L141. Less fighting and a symptom in the same two weeks.
- <a id="C64"></a>**C64** “He started to spend a lot of time with an attractive female "friend" of the family, an activity he did little to hide. The wife soon got preoccupied with her husband's activities again and started berating him for his disloyalty. He shouted back, but gave up the female "friend."” Kerr, FE7 L143.
- <a id="C65"></a>**C65** “His attention to the third person was a transparent but effective means of restoring the old balance in the marriage. The marriage was a pretty tight "conflictual cocoon."” Kerr, FE7 L145. Kerr's reading.

#### Questions

The dated facts: escalating conflict (entry to therapy); two weeks with the wife holding back and migraines, the first since before the marriage; her real change in focus, no headaches; his accusation, then time with the "friend"; berating and shouting; the friend given up. Which of these are conflict events, and on whom? Is the time with the "friend" a toward move to a third person, a triangle move, or both? "Restoring the old balance" is Kerr's reading of motive, kept as a hypothesis (status item 9). The order is in the text; the direction is not tested.

## 5. Readings that look right but are off-theory

|---|---|---|
| The fight is about money, the children or the in-laws, so code the issue. | The issues bring out the reactivity; they do not cause the conflict. Code the move, keep the topic as a note. | C14 |

| Conflict harms the children. | Bowen: "Don't believe it", with a qualifier; impairment of children also happens in calm marriages. | C31, C32 |
