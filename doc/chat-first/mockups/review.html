Ballot and Meeting — three coders code blind, everyone votes on a phone, the meeting sees only what the vote left open
One recorded session, coded from scratch by three people who have not read each other. When the third coding lands, a ballot opens on every coder's phone: one disputed item per screen, the takes shown without names, and nothing from the AI. The meeting then opens with only the items the ballot did not settle, names and counts now shown, and ends when the room ratifies. The AI's own reading arrives afterwards, as an audit against ground truth. Drawn with the app's own stylesheet and markup. Fictional people only.

1b — what “change…” opens.
One disputed moment per screen; tap a take to vote for it as written. “change…” opens the app's own event editor as a sheet over the ballot, prefilled with the item, so you can write a take none of the coders wrote — a different date, different wording, or a different variable — and it joins the tally as one more take. “drop” votes that this should not be a moment in the record at all, because it is not an event, is a duplicate, or is folded into another moment; “not coded” is not a vote, it only says how many coders left the item out. “next” moves to the next item, and an item you skip stays on your list until the ballot closes.

The meeting screen.
The agreement timeline runs across the top: every moment in the cut, in order, one dot each, teal where the vote agreed and amber where it did not, with a small count of different takes beside a disputed dot. Tap a disputed dot to see who wrote what. Below it the disputed moments come first, most split at the top, each with its tally and the three choices. Then the moments everyone agreed on, collapsed, for the room to confirm or reopen. Nothing is settled before the meeting by a count; the room settles every disputed item and the ratify button stays dead until each one has a choice.
