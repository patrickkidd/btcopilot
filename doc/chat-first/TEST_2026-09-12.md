## Walk 1 — the coder's one task

Open the coder link.

1. You land on a card, not on the chat. It names the conversation — "Marcus's move to Arizona" —
   where it is frozen, and how long it should take. Under it is a faint list of what this coder has
   already finished.
2. The vote is open on this conversation, so the button is live and reads "vote" — that is Walk 3,
   and you can come back to it. Before the vote is opened the same button is grey and will not
   press, which is also correct: there is nothing for the coder to do yet.
3. Tap the arrow at the top left. You are on the chat, with the picture above and the sessions
   button at the bottom left.
4. Tap the sessions button. The sheet comes up with the sessions in it, and a button near the
   bottom that takes you back to your task.

What to look for: the card should read as one thing to do, never a list to choose from.

## Walk 3 — the ballot

Go back to the coder window and reload the page.

1. The card's button is now live and says "vote". Tap it.
2. You get one disputed event, filling the screen: the date, who it happened to, what happened, and
   the line from the transcript it came from.
3. Under it are the opinions the coders wrote. They carry no names and no counts: how many coders
   backed an opinion is kept for the meeting. When any coder left the item out, a faint
   line under the opinions says so, in plain words, with no outline and nothing to tap.
4. Above the event is a row of dots, one per event, with the one you are on enlarged and green.
5. Tap an opinion. It records your vote and moves you on.
6. On the next event, tap "change…". The app's own event editor opens over the ballot, already
   filled in, so you can write an opinion nobody wrote. Close it without saving.
7. Under the change and drop buttons is a line asking you to say why you voted as you did. It is
   optional and you can leave it empty.
8. Tap "open in transcript". You are put into the conversation at the line in question, and you
   cannot type into it. The arrow at the top left brings you back to the item you left.
8. Tap "drop" on one event. That is a vote that it should not be in the record at all.
9. Tap "next" to skip one. It should still be waiting for you if you go round again.
10. Before the events come the people and the couples. One of them is Corinne: two coders had her
    born to Marcus and Delphine, one had her born to nobody on the record. Each version is drawn as
    a small family with her in the middle, so you can see the argument as a shape before you read a
    word of it. Another is the couple Marcus and Delphine: two coders had them married, one had
    them together and not married.
11. On one of those, tap "change…". The person's own editor opens over the ballot — the same editor
    the record is corrected in — and saving it joins the count as one more version. Close it
    without saving.

What to look for: whether the drawing tells you what the disagreement is, and whether you can
decide most of this on your own, and whether anything makes you
want to know who wrote an opinion.

## Walk 4 — the meeting

In the admin window, on the agenda screen, tap "run the meeting".

1. At the top, one title line: the word Meeting, the day of the meeting, and the name of the
   conversation. Under it one line of three figures, each labelled: how many events, how many
   disputed, and what agreement was before the vote. Under that the three colours with a word each.
   Nothing on this screen says a figure twice.
2. Under that is a row of dots, one per event, teal where the vote agreed and amber where it did not,
   with a small count beside a disputed one. Tap any dot: it turns green and the list jumps to that
   event's card.
3. Under the dots, the order the list is read in: by divergence, or by time. By divergence the most
   split come first and the agreed ones follow in their own section. By time every event is in one
   list in the order it happened, the agreed ones marked agreed.
4. Every row names who and what. An event with no date says "no date yet". No row reads "an item".
5. The people and the couples are at the top of the list, before the events, each version with its
   own "keep this" and its own drawing. They are not on the row of dots: the dots stay one per
   event, and the line of colours under the figures ends with how many people and bonds the cut
   holds.
6. On a disputed card, each version has its own "keep this" button beside the names behind it, so
   keeping one is one tap. Here, and only here, the opinions carry names and counts. Every coder
   shows the same way, as initials with full stops. Nobody shows as an email address. Below the
   versions, "change…" and "mark unresolved".
7. Tap a row the vote agreed on: it opens the same card, with a close button at its top right.
   "keep this" is greyed out once the room has kept it; change and mark unresolved stay live.
8. Give a choice to the first item and watch the button at the bottom: it counts down how many still
   need a choice.
9. Work through the rest. When the last one has a choice, the ratify button comes alive and reads
   just "ratify".
10. Tap ratify. The button goes dead, reads "ratifying" and shows the app's three dots. It takes
   about eleven seconds, which is the coach writing the guideline changes. A second tap does
   nothing. Wait for the result screen.

What to look for: whether the room could actually be run off this screen, and whether the order the
items come in is the order you would want to take them.

## Walk 5 — what the meeting produced

Ratifying puts you straight onto the result screen.

1. At the top: how many events were ratified, how many were left unresolved, one more count saying
   how many people and bonds the room ratified and how many it left open, agreement on the first
   pass and after ratification side by side, and how the coach's own pass scored against the agreed
   record.
2. Then the guideline changes the AI wrote out of what the room decided. Each one shows the item it
   came from and the margin it was decided by, and carries a "flag for next meeting" link. Tap one
   flag and watch it change to "flagged for next meeting".
3. Then where the AI's reading differed from the room, with its reason, as an audit and not a vote.
4. Then what each coder tends to do differently from the others.

**What happens next in the coder window.** Once this conversation is ratified and there is nothing
new on the agenda, reload the coder window. The one task card still opens. It reads "Nothing is on
the agenda to code yet", and under "done before" is the conversation just ratified. Tap that row and
this result screen opens again. The (i) at the top of the card opens the coding guidelines.

## Walk 6 — the coding guidelines

The guidelines can be read whether or not a coder has an open task, so this walk works at any point.

1. In the coder window, start the task so the conversation opens. If there is nothing on the agenda,
   stay on the one task card instead.
2. At the top of that screen is an (i). It is there on the coding screen and on the task card. Tap
   it.
3. The current coding guidelines slide in. Each rule shows the decision it came from and carries a
   flag link.
4. Tap the back arrow at the top left. You are back where you started, the coding or the card.

## Walk 6b — a person's family, corrected by hand

In any window, tap the list button at the right-hand end of the row under the picture, then People.

1. Tap someone. Under "Family" the editor asks who they were born to: the couples on the record, and
   nobody. The couple that person is themselves part of is not in the list.
2. Somebody with nobody on the record is also offered "add parents". Tap it. Two people appear in
   the list, named after the child — "Corinne's father" and "Corinne's mother" — and the person is
   now born to their couple. The offer is gone, because a person is born to one couple: to change
   it you pick another.
3. Under that is one row per other person they have ever been bonded to, and a row to add one. A row
   says the other person's name, whether they married, and the year it ended when the record has
   one. Tap a row: that bond's own small editor opens in place, asking who the other person is and
   whether they married. When it started and when it ended are events, so they are said to the
   coach and not typed here.
4. The people list itself is unchanged: a name and one faint line. No chips.

What to look for: whether a wrong link — somebody married to their own mother, say — is something
you can see and undo here.
