## Walk 1 — the coder's one task

Open the coder link.

1. You land on a card, not on the chat. It names the conversation, where it is frozen, and how long
   it should take. Under it is a faint list of what this coder has already finished.
2. The button on the card is grey and will not press. That is correct right now: the vote has not
   been opened yet, so there is nothing for this coder to do. Walk 3 comes back here.
3. Tap the arrow at the top left. You are on the chat, with the picture above and the sessions
   button at the bottom left.
4. Tap the sessions button. The sheet comes up with the sessions in it, and a button near the
   bottom that takes you back to your task.

What to look for: the card should read as one thing to do, never a list to choose from.

## Walk 2 — the table, and opening the vote

Open the fixture admin link in a second private window.

1. You land on the chat. Tap the sessions button at the bottom left.
2. In the sheet, tap "The table".
3. You see the meeting date at the top, then what is on the table, then every coder with what they
   are doing: not started, coding, done or voted.
4. Under the coders is a line reading how many are closed out, and a button offering to nudge the
   ones who are not done. Tap the nudge button. A message should confirm it went.
5. Below that is "open the vote". Tap it. It should say the vote is open and then go grey.
6. A new button appears offering to run the meeting on that conversation. Leave it for now.

What to look for: everything Patrick does is on this one screen. If you find yourself wanting a
second admin screen, say so.

## Walk 3 — the ballot

Go back to the coder window and reload the page.

1. The card's button is now live and says "vote". Tap it.
2. You get one disputed event, filling the screen: the date, who it happened to, what happened, and
   the line from the transcript it came from.
3. Under it are the takes the coders wrote. They carry no names and no counts: how many coders
   backed a take is kept for the meeting. Under the takes is one count, of how many coders left the
   item out altogether, because leaving it out is not a vote for any take.
4. Above the event is a row of dots, one per event, with the one you are on enlarged and green.
5. Tap a take. It records your vote and moves you on.
6. On the next event, tap "change…". The app's own event editor opens over the ballot, already
   filled in, so you can write a take nobody wrote. Close it without saving.
7. Under the change and drop buttons is a line asking you to say why you voted as you did. It is
   optional and you can leave it empty.
8. Tap "open in transcript". You are put into the conversation at the line in question, and you
   cannot type into it. The arrow at the top left brings you back to the item you left.
8. Tap "drop" on one event. That is a vote that it should not be in the record at all.
9. Tap "next" to skip one. It should still be waiting for you if you go round again.

What to look for: whether you can settle most of this on your own, and whether anything makes you
want to know who wrote a take.

## Walk 4 — the meeting

In the admin window, on the table screen, tap "run the meeting".

1. At the top are the figures: how many items in this cut, how many still open, how many agreed, and
   what agreement was on the first pass.
2. Under that is a row of dots, one per event, teal where the vote agreed and amber where it did not,
   with a small count beside a disputed one.
3. Then the disputed events, most split first. Here, and only here, the takes carry names and counts.
   Every coder shows the same way, as initials with full stops. Nobody shows as an email address.
4. Below the disputed ones are the items the vote already settled, collapsed, each with a way to
   reopen it.
5. Every open item offers three things: keep a take, change it, or mark it unresolved. Give one of
   them to the first item and watch the button at the bottom: it counts down how many still need a
   choice.
6. Work through the rest. When the last one has a choice, the ratify button comes alive and reads
   just "ratify".
7. Tap ratify. The button goes dead, reads "ratifying" and shows the app's three dots. It takes
   about eleven seconds, which is the coach writing the guideline changes. A second tap does
   nothing. Wait for the result screen.

What to look for: whether the room could actually be run off this screen, and whether the order the
items come in is the order you would want to take them.

## Walk 5 — what the meeting produced

Ratifying puts you straight onto the result screen.

1. At the top: how many events were ratified, how many were left unresolved, agreement on the first
   pass and after ratification side by side, and how the coach's own pass scored against the agreed
   record.
2. Then the guideline changes the AI wrote out of what the room settled. Each one shows the item it
   came from and the margin it was settled by, and carries a "flag for next meeting" link. Tap one
   flag and watch it change to "flagged for next meeting".
3. Then where the AI's reading differed from the room, with its reason, as an audit and not a vote.
4. Then what each coder tends to do differently from the others.

**What happens next in the coder window.** Once this conversation is ratified and there is nothing
new on the table, reload the coder window. The one task card still opens. It reads "Nothing is on
the table to code yet", and under "done before" is the conversation just ratified. Tap that row and
this result screen opens again. The (i) at the top of the card opens the coding guidelines.

## Walk 6 — the coding guidelines

The guidelines can be read whether or not a coder has an open task, so this walk works at any point.

1. In the coder window, start the task so the conversation opens. If there is nothing on the table,
   stay on the one task card instead.
2. At the top of that screen is an (i). It is there on the coding screen and on the task card. Tap
   it.
3. The current coding guidelines slide in. Each rule shows the settle it came from and carries a
   flag link.
4. Tap the back arrow at the top left. You are back where you started, the coding or the card.

## Walk 7 — a professional

Open the professional link in a third private window.

1. Tap the sessions button at the bottom left. The sheet has "new session" and, beside it,
   "Upload a recording" and "+ new note". Tap "Upload a recording" and the sheet gives way to the
   upload sheet rather than stacking on top of it. Back out.
2. Tap "+ new note". A note starts as a session of its own and is listed with the sessions, labelled
   as a note.
3. Tap outside the sheet to put it away, then tap your account mark at the top right.
4. The list of records is headed Cases. A tick marks the one the app is on. Tap another one; the
   chat title row should name the case you switched to and the sessions should be that case's.
5. Now make the window wide, about 1280 across. The events and people drawer should pin open on the
   right of the chat instead of sliding over it. Everything else should be the same screen.

What to look for: the fixture has four cases all named Whitlock and one named Free Diagram. The name
Free Diagram sitting in a professional's case list is worth a decision.
