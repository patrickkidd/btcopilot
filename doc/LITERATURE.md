# Precedent in the literature — the running list

One entry per dilemma or question in the coding and review process, as it comes up. Author,
year, what they did, how it maps here or differs. For a future literature review. The app's
scope is wider and more exploratory than most published inter-rater work (SARF's variables are
richer than a typical codebook), so entries say where the precedent thins out.

## Unresolved items after a meeting (2026-09-12)

- MacQueen, McLellan, Kay, Milstein 1998, "Codebook development for team-based qualitative
  analysis": disagreement is a codebook defect; the fix is to revise the definition with the
  contested item as its example, then re-code. Maps directly to the flagged-rule flow and to
  closing an unresolved event by stating a rule.
- Hruschka et al. 2004, "Reliability in coding open-ended data": iterate code, measure, revise
  codebook, re-code, until agreement stabilises; report the number of rounds. Maps to the
  rolling meetings (each teaches the next, R-0244); differs in that SARF has many more codes
  per unit, so expect more rounds.
- Campbell, Quincy, Osserman, Pedersen 2013, "Coding in-depth semistructured interviews":
  "negotiated agreement" — coders discuss and settle disagreements and both the pre- and
  post-negotiation agreement are reported. Maps to the two agreement figures on the result
  screen (before the ballot, after ratification).
- Krippendorff 2004/2019, Content Analysis: items that cannot be reconciled are recorded with
  the competing readings and excluded from reliability, never forced. Maps to "unresolved kept
  as data, out of the ratified record" (R-0250).
- Aroyo and Welty 2015, "Truth is a lie: crowd truth and the seven myths of human annotation";
  the annotator-disagreement literature since: disagreement is signal about the item and the
  instructions, not noise to be voted away. Maps to keeping every original opinion in fidelity
  (R-0252).
- AI labeling operations (data vendors' adjudication tiers; frontier-lab rubric-and-calibration
  practice, largely unpublished): persistent splits go to a small expert adjudication, the
  rubric is amended with the item as a worked example, and the item joins the calibration set
  new annotators train on. Maps to the meeting as adjudication and the guidelines as the rubric
  the AI amends (R-0259). Thin as citable precedent; note it as practice, not literature.

## Flagged rules (2026-09-12)

- Same MacQueen 1998 and Hruschka 2004 practice: a challenged definition is re-examined against
  the items it was applied to. Early in this study the volume under any one rule is small, so a
  flagged rule is discussed on its wording and its originating event; the "coded under it since"
  view is a later refinement.
