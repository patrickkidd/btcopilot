Addition from Patrick, 2026-10-05 (not a reply to a question; it raises the bar for this job).

His words: "How do I know that the worker won't be lazy in its research? Models tend to just grab a few sources and call it good. But I want to make sure I get the best research and whatever is solid in this domain towards the goal of finding a hook to get people engaged early and keep returning in a stable manner." And: "everything we do needs to be data driven and in a regression suite that measures performance along all these lines. So part of the goal here is to use the research to define what to track and how to track it."

So, on top of the brief:

1. Coverage is shown, not claimed. Add `coverage.md`: one row per field (Bowen theory; psychotherapy alliance and early dropout; role induction and expectations; session-by-session feedback; motivational interviewing; clinical interviewing and pacing as taught in training; digital mental-health and coaching-app attrition; conversational agents in mental health; behavioural psychology of habit and return, including reinforcement schedules, unfinished-task effects, commitment and goal-setting; self-determination and intrinsic motivation; the gambling and consumer-app engagement literature read critically; anything else you find that bears on it). Per row: the search terms tried, the databases and sites searched, how many sources were opened, how many kept, the review articles and meta-analyses the field itself treats as standard, and the strongest contrary finding. A field with fewer than eight sources opened, or with no review or meta-analysis found, says why.
2. Start each field from its reviews and meta-analyses and follow their reference lists to the primary studies; prefer the most cited and the most recent. Stop a field only when two further searches in a row add no new claim, and say so in the row.
3. Add `search-log.md`: every query, where it was run, and what it led to. Short lines.
4. Two independent passes after the first draft, by agents that did not write it: (a) a citation verifier that opens every cited source and marks each claim as supported, overstated or not found; (b) a critic whose only job is to find what is missing or wrong: major authors, findings or whole fields a specialist would expect and the draft lacks, and evidence against the draft's top findings. The draft is revised from both, and `report.md` gives the counts (claims checked, overstated, removed; gaps the critic found and filled).
5. Grade every finding by its evidence and by how far it transfers to this product (one person, text chat, no therapist, family-systems frame). A finding from therapist-led treatment that may not transfer says so.
6. The seed list of measures is a floor. Add every criterion the research turns up, and say which source each comes from.
7. Add `suite.md`, the design of a regression suite for conversational flow: one row per measure with its exact definition, the data it needs (the messages, the stored record, timestamps), how it is computed, which kind of check it is (a fixed-rule test on a made-up conversation; a replayed coach conversation scored k of n; a number tracked over real threads per model and prompt version), a starting threshold or the baseline still to be measured, and the source. Mark the ten you would build first and why. Baselines on real threads will be computed on the coordinator's machine; do not ask for real data.
8. The hook itself: `early-value.md` ends with your three best candidates for what the coach does early that gives a person a reason to return in a stable way, each with its evidence, its fit with Bowen theory, how it would be tested, and the measure in `suite.md` that would show it working.
