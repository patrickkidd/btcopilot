# Job 015: what to measure about users and about the coach, for dashboards

Base ref: branch FD-372 of btcopilot-sources, commit 3271203 (fetch it). Work branch: worker/job-015-dashboards of btcopilot-sources, created from the base. Push only that branch. Nothing from this job goes into btcopilot. Output under research/dashboards/.

## The ask, in Patrick's words (2026-10-06)
"we need all manner of dashboarding for user content and characteristics, and also coach content and characteristics. Like to understand how our coaches are behaving (overlaps heavily with the conversational flow metrics), but also all the things we need to learn about our users to serve them better, and also to get them to return more which overlaps with serving them better. I don't have any idea what those would be. At least what they are interested in talking about? Number of complaints? I have no idea. Probably needs to be a research question for you."

The product: one person typing to a coach that fills the person's family record (people, events with dates, pair-bonds) while talking; the record draws a family picture over time; Bowen family systems theory is the frame. The product problem: people chat for a while and do not come back. No model may be the judge of any measure: every number comes from a fixed rule over the messages, the stored record, the change log, the timestamps, or a count of tool calls. Nothing from real users is available to you; use made-up examples only.

## Do
1. Research, inside and outside the corpus, with the same coverage discipline as job 012 (coverage.md: per field, searches, sources opened and kept, the standard reviews, the strongest contrary finding; search-log.md; a citation-verifier pass and a critic pass by agents that did not write the draft): what product and clinical teams measure about (a) users of a conversational or coaching product to serve them better and keep them returning without addiction-style design (what they talk about, what they ask for, complaints, confusion, drop points, time-of-day and cadence, what they stop answering, what they correct, what they come back to, their stated goal, where they are in the family record's coverage, what the picture shows them), and (b) the coach's behaviour beyond the flow measures (what it asks about, what it asserts, how often it uses the record, errors and refusals, tool calls, reply length and shape, drift between prompt versions). Fields to cover at least: product analytics for conversational apps; digital mental-health engagement and attrition; psychotherapy process research on in-session events; content analysis and topic coding of therapy and coaching talk; complaint and friction detection in chat; clinical quality dashboards and routine outcome monitoring; Bowen theory on what the coach should notice about a person over time.
2. Propose the measure catalogue: catalogue.md, one row per measure: name in plain words; what it tells Patrick; the exact rule (data: messages, record, change log, timestamps, tool calls); the kind of chart (a count over time, a share, a distribution, a table of top items); the source it comes from; its risk of being gamed or misread. Mark the fifteen to build first and why. Topic interest ("what they are interested in talking about") must be a fixed-rule approach (a closed list of life topics mapped from the record's event kinds and the basic-data items, not a model classifier); say how.
3. storage.md: what each build-first measure needs stored that the app does not store today, as a table of proposed rows (the app already has an observations table feeding a quality dashboard in Grafana Cloud; prefer reusing it; a new table or column is Patrick's decision, so present it as a question with the names and one line per column).
4. dashboards.md: three dashboard sketches in words (users; the coach; return), each panel with its measure and the question it answers, ordered by what Patrick would look at first.
5. report.md on the mailbox (at most 10 lines): fields covered with counts, the top five measures for users and the top five for the coach, the storage questions for Patrick, the strongest contrary finding.
